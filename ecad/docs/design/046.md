## 1. Words

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **strip page** | A `terminals` generated page drawn in the `graphic` style |
| **column** | One plan row drawn as one terminal body: a one-level position is one column; each level of a multi-level position is its own column, side by side |
| **band** | The horizontal strip of terminal bodies across the sheet |
| **lead** | A conductor drawn from a column: up for inside, down for outside |
| **cable bar** | A thick horizontal line under the outside leads that are cores of one cable |
| **cable box** | The rectangle under a cable bar naming the cable, with an arrow to its far device |

No glossary change: "terminal", "strip", "level", "link", "bridge", "accessory" are 028/030/036's
words.

## 2. The page style

- A `terminals` page has a **style**: `table` (028, unchanged) or `graphic` (this brief). It is the
  registry entry `terminal.plan.style` (§7.2), resolved **page → project → built-in** (`table`).
  So a project can make every terminal page graphic, and one page can differ.
- Same page kind, same `strip` property (one strip, or every strip in tag order), same rows: both
  styles read `Outputs.strips`, nothing else of lane D.
- Changing the style is 042's `setdefault {key: "terminal.plan.style", level: "page", page, value}`
  (or `reset: true`), one transaction, one `u`. The kind stays fixed (024's `KIND_FIXED`).

## 3. Layout of one strip (A3 landscape; other sheets scale, §6.2)

Coordinates are paper millimetres in the drawing area of a sheet (`generated::area`). The band's
centre line sits at `strippage.band_y` from the area's top (built-in 110 mm, so leads and cable
boxes fit below).

```
   targets (rotated 90°, reading upwards)       ┐
   wire numbers (rotated, beside the lead)      │ inside   (lead up, strippage.lead)
   ─────────────── lead ───────────────────     ┘
 ┌──────┐ ┌─┬─┬─┬─┐┃┌─┬─┬─┐  …                    band: one column per plan row
 │ -X01 │ │1│2│3│4│┃│5│6│7│     labels rotated, place under the label
 │ +CP1 │ └─┴─┴─┴─┘┃└─┴─┴─┘
 └──────┘  │ │ │ │                                ┐
   wire numbers, core labels, targets (rotated)   │ outside  (lead down)
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━━  cable bar  ━━━━━━━━             │
          ┌───┐ cable box: tag, type, part        │
          └─┬─┘                                   │
            ▼ far device tag, description         ┘
```

### 3.1 Columns

- One column per **terminal plan row** of `StripData::plan(true)`, in that order (028 positions,
  036 levels in level order). Gap items (030 accessories) are narrow columns of their own (§5.3).
- Column width `strippage.pitch` (built-in 9 mm). Levels of one position touch; positions touch;
  the strip reads as one rail.
- Body height `strippage.body` (built-in 22 mm). **Levels** are shown by a stagger: level *n* of a
  multi-level position has its top and bottom raised by `(n − 1) × 1.5 mm`, so decks read apart; a
  one-level position is not raised. The position's columns share one outline (`ecad.level-box`)
  around them, 036's position box on the page.

### 3.2 The strip tag box

Left of the first column, a box `strippage.tagbox` wide (built-in 18 mm) and as tall as the band:
the strip tag rotated 90°, the location (`+CP1`) and the counts line under it (`12 positions`). When
the strip has links or wire bridges, a small arrow and the word `links` or `bridges` points at the
lane they use (§5.1), at the box's right edge.

### 3.3 Inside (above the band)

Per conductor in `TermRow.inside` (028 §5.2's side rule, per level):

- a lead `strippage.lead` long (built-in 25 mm) from the column's top centre, `ecad.hairline`,
  with a connection dot where it meets the body (`ecad.dot-size`);
- the **wire number** (`SideCell.wire`) rotated 90°, left of the lead, near the band;
- the **target** (`SideCell.target`: `-K01:A1`, `junction 105-C-4`, `24V >`) rotated 90°, at the
  lead's far end, reading upwards;
- a missing value is 024's `?` with its box (`generated::missing_mark`).

### 3.4 Outside (below the band)

The same, mirrored, from `TermRow.outside` (the out point of a two-point level):

- wire number and target as §3.3; when the conductor is a **cable core**, the core label
  (`SideCell.core`: `BN`, `GNYE`, `3`) is written beside the wire number, `?` when the member has
  none (028);
- **cable bars** (§4.1) below the targets, then **cable boxes** (§4.2).

### 3.5 More than one conductor on one side

Two conductors on one side (028's sub-row `3′`) are two leads at ±1.5 mm from the column centre,
each with its texts. A third and more are not drawn as leads: the column shows `+n` under the
second lead's target, and the table style lists them. A drawn page never hides that they exist.

### 3.6 Labels and the terminal's own place

Inside the body, rotated 90°: the shown label (`TermRow.shown`: `3`, `1.L`, `2.1S`, an override
with no mark), and beside it, smaller, the terminal's place (`TermRow.place`, in the project's
reference style: `105-C-4`), i.e. where the level is drawn in the schematic. A spare position has
no place.

## 4. Cables

### 4.1 Cable bars

- One bar per cable **per sheet**, from the first to the last outside lead on this sheet that is a
  core of it, `strippage.bar` below the targets (built-in 6 mm), line weight `ecad.cable-weight`
  (`ecad.strip.cable-bar`, §13).
- Cables whose bars overlap on a sheet get **lanes** (greedy by first column, then cable tag,
  stable, as 030's link lanes): lane *k* is `k × 2.5 mm` lower. At most `MAX_CABLE_LANES` = 8
  lanes (a guard against hostile files with thousands of crossing cables); further cables share the
  last lane.
- A core of the cable that is not on this strip is not drawn (the cable schedule lists it).

### 4.2 Cable boxes

- One box per bar, under the bar's first column, joined to the bar by a vertical line: a
  rectangle 8 × 24 mm, its texts rotated 90°: the cable **tag** (`?` when it has none, flagged),
  the cable **type** (`CableRow.kind`, `7G1.5`) and its maker or part (`CableRow.maker`).
- Under the box an arrow to the **far device**: the device tag of the cable's far ends
  (`SideCell.far` of its cores, distinct, in tag order, at most 3, then `+n`), and the far device's
  description when it has one (`DeviceRow`), rotated, smaller.
- A cable whose cores run onto the next sheet draws its bar and box on each sheet it is on, the box
  saying `-W01 (cont.)` on the later sheets (Q6).

## 5. Links, bridges, levels and accessories

### 5.1 Links and wire bridges

- **Links** (030, 036 per level): a horizontal line through the column centres of its linked
  columns, with a 1 mm filled dot on each linked column, in the band's **upper half**: lane *k* at
  `band centre + 3 mm + k × 2 mm`. Lanes are `LinkInfo.lane` (030 §5.3), so the page and the plan
  agree. Past skipped columns the line is dashed (`ecad.link`). A cross-level link (036 §5.4)
  is a short vertical bar inside its position box.
- **Continuation:** where a link has positions before the first or after the last column on this
  sheet, its line runs on `ecad.link-stub` (4 mm) past the sheet's first or last column, as on the
  schematic page (030's convention, which the reference's cover notes describe too).
- **Wire bridges** (028: a conductor between two terminals of one strip) draw the same way in the
  band's **lower half**, solid, with `● n`'s group number at the left end; they are conductors, so
  they are not also drawn as leads.
- **Internal bridges** (036 type bridges): a vertical bar joining the bridged levels inside the
  position box.

### 5.2 Kinds of level

| Row | Body |
| --- | --- |
| Drawn | Outline `ecad.strip.body`, label in `ecad.tag` |
| PE (one-level PE, or an L/N/PE level whose level is PE) | Outline plus a 45° hatch at 1 mm (`ecad.strip.pe-hatch`); the label as drawn |
| Free level (036) | Outline, label, `—` instead of leads |
| Spare (028 offer) | Dashed outline, label in `ecad.xref-spare`, no leads, no place |
| Two-point level (fuse, disconnect; 036 §2.2) | Outline with a break mark across the middle: a fuse a 3 × 6 mm box on a line, a disconnect a slanted stroke; the rating (`TermRow.rating`) beside the label |
| Marker on the level (`acc.on.<p>`, 036 §7.4) | A 3 × 1.5 mm open box on the body's top edge |

### 5.3 Gap accessories (030 §7.1 order)

| Accessory | Column width | Drawn as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End stop | 3 mm | Two filled bars, each 1 mm, the band's height + 4 mm |
| End plate | 1.5 mm | One filled bar, the band's height |
| Partition | 2 mm | One filled bar, the band's height + 6 mm |
| Strip label | 8 mm | A box with the strip tag rotated |

A link line passes over a gap column (a partition does not stop it on the page; 030's
`link-across-partition` check already warns).

## 6. Paging long strips

### 6.1 Sheets of one strip

- Each strip starts a new sheet (as the table style). The strips are those `strips_shown` gives
  (the page's `strip`, else every strip in tag order).
- A sheet holds columns until the next **position** (all its levels, and the gap columns that follow
  it) would pass the area's right edge less `strippage.margin` (built-in 10 mm); then the next sheet
  starts. **A position is never split**; a single position wider than a sheet (a hostile 999-level
  position cannot exist: 036 caps levels by type) still goes on one sheet.
- With the built-ins on A3 that is about 38 one-level columns per sheet.
- The sheet count of a strip is kept in `StripPages` (§9), from the column widths only (O(rows of
  that strip), when the strip changed).

### 6.2 Other sheet sizes

The layout uses the area's width and height as they are: a smaller sheet holds fewer columns. When
the area is less than 160 mm tall, `band_y` and `lead` scale down with it (`area height / 260`),
never below 0.5 of their values, and the texts keep their style heights (§7.1).

### 6.3 Titles and numbers

Each sheet's title is the page title, then the strip tag and its description, then the sheet within
that strip: `Terminal strips: -X01 Interface (2/3)` (Q5). Page numbers, Next and the book's Total
follow 033's sheet numbering, unchanged (`sheet_number`).

## 8. Pages and Publish

### 8.1 Making one

- **Pages ▸ New page ▸** gains **Terminal strips** after **Terminal plan**
  (`ecad.project.newpage.terminals-graphic`). It runs
  `newpage {generated: "terminals", style: "graphic"}` and opens the page; the title defaults to
  `Terminal strips`. The `strip` choice is the same as New page ▸ Terminal plan's.
- `newpage` gains `style` (`"table"` | `"graphic"`), allowed only with `generated: "terminals"`;
  otherwise refused: `` `style` is for a terminal plan page (`generated: "terminals"`) ``. It writes
  the page-level `terminal.plan.style` in the AddPage's properties, so the page is one transaction.
- `setdefault {key: "terminal.plan.style", level: "page", page, value}` switches an existing page;
  the registry checks the value. Settings ▸ Standards (042) and the Inspector's page rows list it
  through the registry, with no new control.

### 8.2 States

| State | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| No strips | One sheet, the band area says `No terminal strips yet.` (028's words) |
| The `strip` property names a deleted device | One sheet, `?` flagged, as 028 |
| A strip with no drawn terminals, only an offer | Every column spare (dashed) |
| Read-only (revision open, writes off) | Drawn the same; the page is generated, so nothing on it is ever editable (024) |

### 8.3 Pages panel and Publish

- A strip page is a page like any other in Pages (number, folder, move, delete); its sheet count
  shows in the number range (`500–502`), as 028's.
- Print Book and Release print it with no change: they call `generated::parts_of_pending`, which
  dispatches by style. Missing values print as 033's ink `?` box. Ink only (§13).
- Generated `?` count (Print Book's warning line, `generated::flagged`): unchanged; graphic pages
  count like table pages (028 counts none for `terminals`; this brief keeps that).

## 9. Update map

`StripPages` (in `strip_page.rs`, kept in `Outputs` beside `strips`) holds per strip: its column
widths' sheet count (`sheets`), and two indexes the drawing reads beyond `StripData`:
**`by_cable: CableId → strips`** (cables with a core on the strip) and **`by_far: ComponentId →
strips`** (far devices named in a cable box). It is built whole where `Strips` is (open, reload,
branch switch, revision open, first show). After that `StripPages::update(doc, ops, &strips)` runs
right after `Strips::update` in `Outputs::update`. **Every op not listed does nothing.**

| Op or change (kind, target) | Recomputed |
| --- | --- |
| a strip in `Strips::last_changed` (every 028/030/036 row change: terminals, wires, numbers, cables' membership and tags, accessories, links, labels, levels) | that strip: sheet count, indexes; it is in `StripPages::last_changed` |
| `SetProperty(Cable k, type \| maker \| cores)` | strips in `by_cable[k]`: marked changed (box texts), no sheet count change |
| `SetProperty(Component d, description)`, `d` in `by_far` | strips in `by_far[d]`: marked changed |
| `SetProperty(Project, strippage.*)` | every strip with graphic pages shown: sheet counts (a project layout setting; O(strips), same as a full layout, allowed as a settings change like 031's text sizes) |
| `SetProperty(Page P, terminal.plan.style)` | P's sheet count (from the kept per-strip counts of the other style or this one) and P redrawn; `frame::touches` already names P's own property |
| `SetProperty(Project, terminal.plan.style)` | the `terminals` pages without a page value: their sheet counts; the shown one redrawn |
| text style and font keys (`text.*`) | nothing here: `frame::touches` already redraws the shown page; sheet counts do not depend on text |
| anything else | nothing |

**Redraw.** `generated::terminals_rewritten(doc, page, o)` answers for a `terminals` page:
table style, 028's rule (`strips.last_changed`); graphic, `strip_pages.last_changed` ∩ the strips
it shows. `project.rs`'s `rewritten` closure calls it (its one hunk). The shown page redraws only
then; other open pages are marked stale (PR #75's rule). The book's Total moves only when a kept
sheet count changes (`update_outputs` compares `sheets()`, which now reads the style's kept count).

**Verify mode.** `StripPages` equals a rebuild after every command (like `verify_strips`); a
counter `StripPages::last_work` names how many strips the last update laid out.

## 10. Cost and bench (`cargo xtask bench`; medium while building, large once at the end)

Layout of one strip is O(its rows): columns, lanes (rows log rows) and cable bars. Drawing one sheet
is O(its columns + its leads + its texts), about 40 columns and 200 texts.

| Row | Measures | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `spage ms` | first draw of one graphic sheet (38 columns, 20 cables) | < 100 ms (as `tplan`) |

| `spageoff ms` | the same on a strip not shown | `stripupd` + < 1 ms; no projection (counter) |

## 12. Format (for the overseer)

**No FP item.** No new op, record field or page property name: the style is the registry entry
`terminal.plan.style` at page and project level, stored by 031's registry as an ordinary property
under its own key, as every registry entry is. An old build reads a graphic page as a `terminals`
page with an unknown property and draws the table: nothing breaks. **Overseer, 2026-11-13:
confirmed.** Registry keys stored at page level need no FP; 031 §3.5 approved the mechanism.

   ▸ Terminal strips makes a graphic page directly, and your projects can set the project default to
   `graphic` once.
3. **One page for every strip** (each from its own sheet, live as strips are added) as the main
   way, with `strip` for a page of one strip? **Recommend yes**: a new strip appears in the book with
   no page to add; per-strip pages stay possible when you want fixed page numbers.
4. **Inside above, outside below**, from 028's side rule, never configured per page? **Recommend
   yes**: it is the reference's convention and 028's rule already decides each conductor's side.
5. **Sheet titles** `<page title>: -X01 <description> (2/3)`, counted within the strip, not across
   the page? **Recommend yes**: the reader of a strip page thinks in strips.
6. **A cable running onto the next sheet** draws its bar and box on each sheet, `(cont.)` on the
   later ones? **Recommend yes**: each printed sheet is readable alone.
7. **More than two conductors on one side** shown as `+n` (the table lists them)? **Recommend
   yes**: three leads in a 9 mm column cannot be read.
8. **Ink only**, no colour per text role (the reference colours wire numbers, targets and cores)?
   **Recommend ink only now**: 040 prints generated pages in mono; colour per role belongs to
   1.13's text-style catalogue (a style with a colour), which then applies to every page at once.
9. **Existing text kinds only** (§7.1), no new strip kinds? **Recommend yes**: sizes then follow the
   styles you already set; 1.13's named styles can split them later without a format change.
10. **Far device under the cable box**: tag and description, at most three, `+n` after?
    **Recommend yes**: it is what the box is for; the schedule has the rest.
11. **Partitions do not stop link lines on the page** (030 warns)? **Recommend yes**: the page shows
    what is stored; the check says it is odd.
12. **No click-through** from a strip page to the terminal in this brief? **Recommend yes**: it is UI
    polish (later, with the terminal manager 2.6), and the brief adds no panels.
