### 0.2 What the code does today (checked on `6729c8f1`)

- `run_wire` (`commands.rs`): for each end, if no connection point is there, `wires::wire_at`
  finds a wire under the end and `plan_junction` splits it. `plan_junction` refuses a route end
  (`Click on a wire, not its end…`), and `run_wire` drops the refusal (`let Ok(j) = … else
  { continue }`). **That is the Q6 bug**: an end on another wire's loose end stays loose, gets a new
  net and number, and the command reports success.
- `junction {at}` with two or more loose ends at the point and no wire through it places a junction
  and attaches them (the pack's workaround, `attached: 2`).
- Overlay (`interact/mod.rs` `overlay_marks`): a 6 px grey square (`LOOSE_ACI` 9) on every loose
  end, none where an end is `quiet` (a cable reference). Two coincident loose ends look like one
  deliberate open end. Overlay marks are not printed.
- Checks (`checks.rs`): **Open end**, **Loose wire**, **Looks connected, no junction** (fix
  `Fix::AddJunction`) already exist and are kept by the update map of design 022 §11.
- A cable (`Cable { properties, members: Vec<CableMember { conductor, core } > }`) has one slash
  representation (role `slash`). `cableslash` always creates a new cable and tag; a used tag is
  refused by `devices::new_cable_tag`. `cablecontinue` draws the same conductors on another page
  (010) and skips members attached at both ends (`Skip::BothConnected`), so a cable whose cores end
  on continuation arrows cannot continue (`None of cable -W04's wires can continue…`).
- `MAX_REFERENCES_PER_CABLE = 2` (`ecad-model/src/derive.rs`). Not the blocker in the pack: W04
  crossed only two pages. The blocker is that its cores end on continuations.
- Labels (`wires.rs` `labels_on_page_with`): one per Drawn route per page on its longest segment;
  placement avoids labels and wires. Height `wiring.label_height()` (default 1.8 mm).

## 1. Goal and words

Wires that look joined are joined; a cable keeps its tag and cores across pages it crosses through
continuations; labels say each net's number once where it helps, not on every stub.

Words (no glossary change proposed; "join" is ordinary English):

| Word | Means here |
| --- | --- |
| **join** | Two wire ends that meet become one wire (one conductor). Three or more ends meet at a junction. |
| **open end** | A wire end attached to nothing, on purpose (existing word, Checks). |
| **looks connected** | An open end lying on another wire's end or body (existing Checks kind). |
| **core chain** | The members of one cable that are one core, joined end to end through continuation pairs (§3.2). Shown to users as "core"; "chain" appears only in code and this design. |

## 2. Q6: wire join

### 2.1 Rules, per end of the new wire W (in this order)

Let P be the end's point on the active page.

1. **A connection point at P** (device, terminal, junction or continuation, within the project
   tolerance): attach. Unchanged.
2. Otherwise count what meets at P (within `ON_WIRE`, 0.5 mm): **L** = open ends of other wires
   drawn on this page; **T** = wires whose interior passes through P (a vertex in the middle of a
   route is interior).
   | L | T | Result |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 0 | 0 | open end (deliberate; grey square as today) |
   | 1 | 0 | **merge** (§2.2): W and that wire become one wire |
   | 0 | 1 | junction on that wire (today, design 022 §3.1) |
   | L + T ≥ 2 | | **one junction at P** joining W's end, every open end there, and the through-wire split at P (the nearest, if T ≥ 2; the others stay crossings, which never connect, and Checks lists them) |
3. **Reverse T (owner Q2).** Every open end of another wire that lies on W's interior splits W
   there with a junction and attaches to it. W becomes several conductors with copied properties,
   as `plan_junction` does today. At most `MAX_WIRE_POINTS` such splits per wire (the existing
   point cap); more is refused (`This wire crosses more than 1000 wire ends; draw it in parts.`).

With `join: false` (JSON) or Join off (Wire tool), steps 2 and 3 are skipped: only step 1 runs, as
`junction: false` does today.

### 2.2 Merge

W's end E_W meets the open end E_C of wire C at P.

- **When it applies:** C has exactly one route, on this page, and is not a cable member. Otherwise
  (a cable core, or a wire drawn on two pages, 010) the end gets a **junction** at P with two wires
  (the Checks Info row "Junction with fewer than three wires" says so).
- **Ops (one transaction, label `Wire`):** no `CreateConductor` for W. `ModifyItem` of C's route:
  C's vertices and W's vertices, oriented so C keeps its End letters (`E_C` moves to W's far end).
  A vertex at P is dropped when the two segments meeting there are collinear (one straight line,
  one longest segment for the label). W's far end then follows §2.1 as C's end `E_C`
  (`AttachEnd { C, E_C, None → t }`, a further merge, or open).
- **Both ends of W meet open ends of C1 and C2 (C1 ≠ C2):** one wire. The survivor is the one with
  a fixed wire number, else C1 (at W's end A). The other is folded in: its far-end attachment is
  re-made on the survivor (`AttachEnd` detach then attach), its route vertices appended to the
  survivor's route, then `UnlinkRepresentation`, `RemoveItem` and `DeleteConductor` for it (it is
  never a cable member, by the first bullet). Its ID is reported in `removed`.
- **Properties:** the survivor keeps its own. Refused, nothing written, when the merge would lose
  or contradict a value (the reasons of `wire_cmds::merge_sense`, extended):
  - `That wire carries 0V and this one 24V; joining them would short them. Draw it with join off
    (join: false), or end it on a connection point.` (W's `potential`, or the two nets' potentials);
  - `Both wires have fixed numbers of their own (24V-03 and 24V-07). …same way out…`;
  - `The two wires have different cross-sections (1.5 and 2.5). …` (and colours), only when both
    are stored on the conductors;
  - `Both ends land on the ends of one wire: that would close it into a loop. …`.
- Net, number and labels follow from the ops through the existing pass (022): the merged wire has
  one net, one number, one label.

### 2.3 `wire` JSON

`wire {points, potential?, join?: bool (true), junction?: bool}`. `junction` is the old name of
`join`: given alone it means `join`; both given and different is refused (`` `join` and
`junction` disagree; give only `join` ``).

Result (new fields in **bold**; old fields keep their meaning):

```json
{"conductor": "<the wire the drawn route now belongs to>", "item": "<its route item>",
 "created": true,
 "joined": [{"end": "A", "conductor": "<C>", "how": "merged"}],
 "removed": ["<C2>"],
 "attached": [...], "junctions": [...], "potential": null, "number": "24V-03"}
```

- **`created`**: false when W was merged into an existing wire (then `conductor` is that wire).
- **`joined`**: one entry per end that merged (`how: "merged"`) or met at a junction with two or
  more others (`how: "junction"`, also listed in `junctions` as today).
- **`removed`**: conductors folded into the survivor (absent when none).
- **`splits`** (reverse T): `[{"at": [x,y], "junction": hex, "conductor": hex}]` (absent when none).

### 2.4 The Wire tool

- Prompts gain a **Join** keyword: `Specify first point of wire [Potential/Join]:` and `Specify
  next point [Undo/Join]:`. It toggles joining for the rest of this tool session (remembered like
  the potential, `Project::wire_join`, default on), and echoes `Joining is off: wire ends stay
  open where they meet other wires.` / `Joining is on.`. While off, the prompt reads
  `Specify next point (join off) [Undo/Join]:`.
- Preview at the cursor (existing `preview_objects`): over an open end, a ring and the text
  `join` in `ecad.attach-preview`; over a wire's interior, `junction` (today); over two or more
  ends, `junction`; with Join off, no ring and no text. Over an end whose merge would be refused
  (§2.2), the ring and the first words of the reason (`0V ≠ 24V`) in `ecad.refuse-preview`.
- Echo after the wire: `Wire 24V-03 drawn; end A joins wire 24V-03 (now one wire), end B attached
  to -K01:A1.` For a junction: `…; end A meets 2 wires at a junction.` A refusal is echoed as the
  command's error and nothing is drawn; the tool keeps its points so the user can type `J` and
  finish.

### 2.5 `joinwires {at}` (new; repairs what already meets)

On the active page. At `at` (within `ON_WIRE`):
- exactly two open ends and no wire through the point: **merge** them (§2.2 rules and refusals,
  the wire with a fixed number survives, else the lower conductor ID);
- an open end on a wire's interior, or three or more ends: a **junction**, as `junction {at}`;
- nothing to join: `There are no wire ends to join here.`

Result: `{"how": "merged" | "junction", "conductor": hex, "removed": [hex]?, "junction": hex?,
"at": [x,y]}`. Label `Join wires`; interactive: click points until Enter (prompt `Click where wire
ends meet:`), like `junction`. Menu: ECAD ▸ Wires ▸ **Join Wires**, next to Junction; Tool Sets
ECAD tab. No shortcut.

### 2.6 What the page shows

- **Looks-connected mark** (new): where an open end lies on another wire's end or interior (the
  Checks condition, excluding `quiet` ends), a ring of 10 px, stroke 1.5 px, in
  `ecad.check-warning` (yellow), drawn over the grey square. Overlay only, not printed. With §2.1
  this happens only after `join: false`, a move or stretch, or in an older file. Hovering the end
  adds the lens line `Open end: looks connected to 24V-03; Join Wires joins them.`
- **Where it is computed:** `wires::looks_connected_on_page(doc, page) -> Vec<Pt>`, during the
  page's projection (as labels are), with a per-page grid of route segments; kept in the page map
  beside `quiet` (`project.rs`), read by `overlay_marks`. Only the page on screen is projected.
- A deliberate open end keeps the grey square (owner answer Q3 to design 008: neutral, not red).

### 2.7 Checks

- **Looks connected, no junction** is renamed **Looks connected** (two open ends at one point are
  joined by merging, not a junction). Its fix button becomes **Join** (`Fix::Join(at)` →
  `joinwires {at}`). `Fix::AddJunction` stays for any other row that uses it.
- **Open end** and **Loose wire** are unchanged. The repro after the fix produces none of them;
  `join: false` produces Looks connected plus two Open end rows.

## 3. Q8: a cable across pages through continuations

### 3.1 The case (pack, W04 and W05, generic)

Page 100: wires from `-X01:1…3` run right to continuation arrows `A1`, `A2`, `A3`; a slash across
them is cable `-W04`, cores 1–3. Page 115: arrows `A1…A3` and wires on to `-X05:1…3`. The user
wants `-W04` slashed on page 115 too, and the cable list to read `-X01 → -X05`.

Decision: **a cable may be slashed on more than one page; on each page its members are that page's
wires, and the members joined through continuation pairs are one core.** This is how the pack and
common practice draw it (a cable definition line on each page), and it needs no new object.
Rejected: continuing the same conductors with `cablecontinue` (the cores are attached to arrows on
page 100, so a second route on page 115 would start nowhere), and raising
`MAX_REFERENCES_PER_CABLE` (not the blocker; question 22 keeps more references in M2).

### 3.2 Core chains (derived, never stored)

Two members M and N of one cable are in one **core chain** when an end of M is attached to a
continuation K, an end of N to a continuation K′, K and K′ have the same name key and are on
different pages, and no other member of this cable is attached to that name group. (A group that
reaches three or more members of one cable links none of them: each stays its own core, and Checks
shows **Core used by two wires** if their labels agree.) Chains are found per cable from its
members' ends (`references::core_chains(doc, cable) -> Vec<Vec<ConductorId>>`), bounded by
`MAX_CABLE_MEMBERS`, cost proportional to the cable's members and their continuations' name groups.

A chain is shown as one core: its label is its members' label (copied when continued, §3.3); its
**from** is the first member's end that is not a continuation, its **to** the last member's.

### 3.3 `cableslash` continuing a cable

`cableslash {from, to, tag?, cable?}`:
- `cable` (an ID), or a `tag` that is an existing cable's tag → **continue mode**. A `tag` used by a
  device is refused as today.
- The crossed wires (today's `slash::found`) must each be joined, through a continuation on this
  page, to a member of the cable whose chain has no member drawn on this page yet, one wire per
  chain. Then, in one transaction (label `Cable slash`): `AddCableMember` for each crossed wire with
  that chain's core label (or none), and `LinkRepresentation` of a `slash` representation of the
  cable on this page with the slash item. No `CreateCable`, no new tag.
- Refused, nothing written:
  - `-W04 already has a slash on this page.`
  - `Wire 24V-03 does not continue a core of -W04: none of its ends meets -W04 through a
    continuation.` (one line per wire, at most 6, then `+n`);
  - `Two of the wires continue -W04's core 2.`
  - existing refusals (a wire already in a cable, too many members).
- Result: today's fields plus `"continued": true` and `"cores": [{"conductor": hex, "core": "2",
  "continues": hex}]`.
- Interactive: the Cable Slash tool's tag prompt accepts an existing cable's tag and previews the
  slash in `ecad.attach-preview` with `continues -W04`, or in `ecad.refuse-preview` with the first
  refusal's words.

### 3.4 `cablecontinue`'s refusal

When every candidate member is skipped as connected at both ends and at least one of those ends is
a continuation, R6 reads: `-W04's cores end on continuations. To show -W04 where they continue,
slash the wires there with tag -W04.` Other cases keep today's R6.

### 3.5 What else follows

| Place | Today | With chains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cable row (`outputs.rs` `CableRow.cores`) | `members.len()` | number of chains |
| Cable core list (028 §6.3, `cablecores`) | one row per member | one row per chain: core, wire number, from (first end), to (last end) |
| Cable list from / to | locations of devices at members' ends | unchanged: it already reads every member's ends and skips continuations, so `-X01 → -X05` follows |
| Checks **Core used by two wires** | two members with one label | two **chains** with one label |
| Checks **More cores used than offered** | members > offer | chains > offer |
| Cable views (fan, traditional, look) | per slash on its page | per slash on its page; members not routed on the page are not drawn there. The worker confirms `look.rs` already behaves so (members of a 010 continued cable are partly off-page); if not, the overseer coordinates with lane T |
| Erasing a slash | deletes the cable | erasing one of several slashes removes that representation and the members routed only on its page (`RemoveCableMember`); erasing the last slash deletes the cable as today |
| Cable references (010) | — | unchanged; a cable may still have at most two |

## 4. Q4: wire labels

### 4.1 Setting (FP-W1)

`WiringSettings` gains:
- `label_rule`: `"net"` (one label per net per page) or `"each"` (one per wire, today). Default
  `"net"` (owner Q6): labels are derived, so an older file only looks tidier, nothing is rewritten.
- `label_min_length`: millimetres, 0 to 100, default 0. Used by `"each"` only (§4.2).

### 4.2 Which routes get a label

On one page, among the routes that are drawn today (Drawn form, not hidden in a fan view), with
their label text from `wiring::label` (`<number> <cross-section> <colour>`):

- `"net"`: group the routes by (net, label text). Each group gets **one** label, on the route whose
  longest segment is longest (ties: lower conductor ID, then lower representation ID). So a net
  whose wires all read `24V-03 0.5 BU` is labelled once per page; a wire of that net with its own
  cross-section is labelled too (owner Q7).
- `"each"`: every route whose longest segment is at least `label_min_length`; a net with no such
  route on the page still gets one label, on its longest route.
- In both: **every net drawn on a page is labelled on that page at least once.** Placement is
  unchanged (candidates, clearance from labels and wires, design 022 §7 and owner 2026-11-09).
- Visibility per wire (hide one label) is lane T's Q3, if the owner places it there (Q8): the label
  pass skips a conductor whose wire-number text lane T's getter reports hidden, and the net's next
  route takes the label.

### 4.3 Height

`labels_on_page_with` reads its height through **lane T's text-style getter** (031), for the wire
label kind. Until 031 lands it reads `wiring.label_height()` as today. FP-W4 asks that the getter
falls back to `wiring.label_height` for wire labels, so a project's existing setting is kept.

### 4.4 Project Settings

In the Project Settings dialog's **Wires** group (`sheets_ui.rs`), under Label height:

```
Labels    (•) One per net per page   ( ) Every wire
          Shortest labelled wire [ 0  ] mm      ← enabled only with Every wire
```

Element names: `ecad.projectsettings.label_rule.net`, `ecad.projectsettings.label_rule.each`,
`ecad.projectsettings.label_min_length`. Invalid length: `Shortest labelled wire is 0 to 100 mm.`
(OK disabled, as Label height today). Read-only project: shown, disabled.

## 12. Acceptance checks (from the outside)

Model and commands (`ecad-cadcraft` tests, `tests/all/wires_nets_contracts.rs`):
1. **Regression (fails on `6729c8f1`):** `wire [[0,0],[50,0]]`, `wire [[50,0],[50,-20]]` → one
   conductor; second result `created: false`, `joined[0].how = "merged"`; `nets` lists one net with
   that conductor; the route has vertices (0,0) (50,0) (50,−20).
2. Collinear: `wire [[0,0],[50,0]]`, `wire [[50,0],[80,0]]` → one conductor, route (0,0)–(80,0)
   (middle vertex dropped).
3. Both ends: three wires drawn A, C, then B between their open ends → one conductor, `removed`
   lists C; C's far attachment (a connection point) is on the survivor.
4. Fixed numbers: survivor is the fixed one; two different fixed numbers → refused, nothing
   written (transaction count unchanged).
5. Potentials 0V and 24V → refused with the words of §2.2.
6. Loop → refused.
7. Three ends at one point → one junction with three attached ends.

## 14. Owner questions (each with a recommendation)

1. **End to end: one wire or two?** When a wire starts on another's open end, merge them into **one
   wire** (one conductor, one wire-list row, one label), or keep two wires joined by a junction
   drawn without a dot? **Recommend one wire.** It is what was drawn, the wire list stays right, and
   it halves the labels.
2. **Reverse T.** An existing open end lying on the middle of the new wire also joins it, with a
   junction. **Recommend yes**: the same silent disconnection otherwise happens in the other
   drawing order.
3. **The way out.** `join: false` on `wire` (keeping `junction: false` as its old name) and a
   **Join** keyword in the Wire tool, remembered for the tool session. **Recommend yes**, with
   joining on by default every time the tool starts.
4. **Continuing a cable by its tag.** Slashing with an existing cable's tag continues it when the
   wires meet its cores through continuations, and is refused otherwise; `cable` (ID) does the
   same. **Recommend yes, both.** The pack's agent typed the tag; refusing it helped nobody.
5. **Strict core matching.** Only wires joined through a **continuation pair** to a core's free end
   continue a core; wires reaching a core through a device or terminal do not. **Recommend
   strict.** A terminal in between means a different conductor in the real panel.
6. **Label rule default.** `"net"` for every project, including older ones (labels are derived;
   nothing is rewritten), or `"each"` for older projects and `"net"` only for new ones?
   **Recommend `"net"` for all**; v0.15 is about how drawings look, and the setting switches it
   back.
7. **Different label text in one net.** With `"net"`, a wire of the net with its own cross-section
   or colour gets its own label. **Recommend yes**: otherwise the 2.5 mm² supply wires on the
   pack's page 100 would again print as the potential's 1.5.
8. **Hiding one wire label.** Is per-wire label visibility part of lane T's Q3 (show and hide per
   text, which lists "wire number") or of Q4 here? **Recommend lane T's Q3**, with one getter both
   lanes read, so all show-and-hide works one way.
9. **Older files with open ends that meet.** Repair them on open, or leave them to Checks and the
   yellow mark? **Recommend leave them**: never rewrite a drawing on open; the Checks list has a
   Join button per point.
