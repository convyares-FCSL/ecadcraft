## 1. Goal and words

A designer builds the PLC as it is bought and wired: a CPU or head module with cards in slots, each
card's channels drawn on the page where their circuit is, with pin numbers, addresses filled in by
slot and channel, spare channels shown and listed, and an I/O list that is always up to date.

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **rack** | A CPU or a head (interface) module and the cards in its slots. The rack **is** the head device: no new object |
| **slot** | A numbered place in a rack. A card is in at most one slot |
| **card** | A device in a slot (or in none yet): an input, output, analogue, RTD, IO-Link, power or communication module. Its type comes from its card body |
| **card type** | DI, DQ, AI, AO, RTD, IOL, PS, PM, CM (§3) |
| **channel** | One input or output of a card: one or more **channel points**, one per **role** |
| **role** | What a channel point is: `in`, `out`, `+`, `-`, `I+`, `S+`, `S-`, `I-`, `L+`, `C/Q`, `L-` (§4.1) |
| **pin** | The label printed at a channel point and typed after the tag (`-A02:4`): the point's terminal label |
| **channel number** | The channel's index shown from the card's first number (0 or 1, §12): `channel 3` |
| **address** | What the PLC program calls the channel: automatic by slot and channel (`I2.3`, `%IX2.3`, `I:3/03`), or typed |
| **spare** | A channel with no wire and no description. **Reserved**: no wire, but a description |
| **I/O list** | One row per channel of every card: rack, slot, card, channel, address, pins, description, what it is wired to, wire number, place |

The glossary gains a section "PLC racks and cards (brief 045)" with these rows.

## 2. Model (a): racks, slots and cards

### 2.1 No new object

- A **rack** is the device whose head is a rack body: `plccpu` (034) or `plcim` (§3.1).
- A **card** is a device whose head is a card body (`plcdi`, `plcdq`, `plcai`, `plcao`, `plcrtd`,
  `plciol`, `plcps` from 034; `plcpm`, `plccm` from §3.1), or a device made only of channel
  symbols (a card without a body: its type from its channel symbols, §4.2).
- A card is **in a slot** when it has both properties (FP1):

| Key (on the card) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `plc.rack` | the rack device's ID, 32 hex digits (`ComponentId::hex`) |
| `plc.slot` | the slot number, 0–999, digits only |

Why the ID, not the rack's tag: renumbering or retagging the rack then changes nothing on its
cards, and two branches that retag the rack still merge. Every command, table, echo and MCP reply
shows the rack's **tag**; only the file holds the ID. A card with one of the two keys, or a
`plc.rack` that is not a rack in this project, is a Checks row (§9), never a refusal on open.

### 2.2 Slots of a rack

A rack has `plc.slots` slots numbered from `plc.slot.first` (registry, §12: 32 and 1, rack level
allowed). The rack body itself takes no slot. Slots are listed in number order; an empty slot is
`free`. More than one card in a slot, or a slot outside the range, is a Checks row with a fix (§9).
A remote I/O station is a rack of its own (its head is a `plcim`); racks do not nest (Q2).

Deleting a rack device (018's delete) also removes `plc.rack` and `plc.slot` from its cards in the
same transaction (Q1): the cards stay, in no rack, and the echo says so (`Deleted -A01; 6 cards are
now in no rack.`). Undo puts them back.

### 2.3 Channel count

A card has `plc.channels` channels (card property, 0–256), else the registry's
`plc.<type>.channels` (DI 16, DQ 16, AI 4, AO 4, RTD 4, IOL 4; PS, PM and CM have none). The count
is what is bought: channels not drawn are still listed (spare or reserved). It never limits drawing
silently: a channel drawn beyond the count is a Checks row with **Raise to n** (§9).

### 2.4 Placing a card body (Place Symbol, 018 §5.3, extended)

When the project has at least one rack and the symbol is a card body:

```
Rack [None] <-A01>:                 (the rack of the last card placed, else the only rack, else None)
Slot <3>:                            (the next free slot of that rack)
Insertion point:
```

Enter takes the defaults; `None` places the card out of any rack. A named slot that is taken is
refused with the way out: `Slot 3 of -A01 holds -A04 (DI 16). Pick another slot, or Enter for the
next free one (5).` One transaction: 018's place ops, plus `SetProperty(card, plc.rack)` and
`SetProperty(card, plc.slot)`. Echo: `Placed -A05 (DI card) in -A01 slot 5.` Placing a `plccpu` or
`plcim` makes a rack; no prompt.

### 2.5 Commands

- **`plcslot {card, rack?, slot?, swap?}`** puts a card in a slot, moves it, or (`rack: null`)
  takes it out. `rack` absent: the card's own rack, else the only rack, else refused `Name a rack:
  the project has 2 (-A01, -A20).` `slot` absent: the next free slot. A taken slot is refused as
  in §2.4 unless `swap: true`, which swaps the two cards' slots in one transaction. One
  transaction; echo `-A05: -A01 slot 5 → slot 2 (addresses of 3 cards change).` The echo counts
  the cards whose automatic addresses move (§5), so nobody is surprised.
- **`plcracks {rack?}`** (read-only, writes off allowed): each rack with its tag, body, address
  style and starts, and its slots in order: `{slot, card, tag, type, channels, used, spare,
  reserved, inputs: [start, end], outputs: [start, end], pinned, place}`; cards in no rack in a
  last group `unracked`. This is the data of the rack overview (UI spec screen m), which the PLC
  manager (2.8) draws; this design draws no overview.

### 2.6 Inspector (042) and the page

Properties ▸ **Card** on a card body or channel symbol: **Rack** (choice of the project's racks and
None, `ecad.props.card.rack`), **Slot** (number, `ecad.props.card.slot`), **Channels** (number,
placeholder the registry value, `ecad.props.card.channels`). Each edit is one `plcslot` or
`setprop` transaction. Properties ▸ **Rack** on a rack body: **Slots**, **First slot**, **Address
style**, **Input start**, **Output start** (registry entries at object level, written with
`setdefault`, shown with where the value comes from as 042 does).

The card body's **caption** (§7.2) shows the rack and slot (`-A01 slot 2`) under the tag.

### 3.2 A card's type

From its head's starter (the table above). A card without a body takes the type of its first
channel symbol (§4.2). A card whose channel symbols are of another type than its body is a Checks
row (`channel-type`, §9); the body's type wins.

## 4. Channels (b)

### 4.1 Channel symbols: cells

Each card type with channels gets one **channel cell**: one channel drawn as a cell of a card box,
so cells placed side by side read as the card, as in the pack. Appended to `symbols.rs`,
`Family::Plc`, `Kind::Channel(type)`, IDs `pc(12)`…`pc(17)`, drawn new for ECAD (034 §2.1's
rules: mm, y up, points on the 5 mm grid, no arcs):

| Key | Name (aliases) | Width | Points (role key at) | Mark (fixed graphics) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `chdi` | PLC digital input channel (plc, di, input, channel) | 10 | `.in` (0,5) | an arrowhead into the card at (0,−8) |
| `chdq` | PLC digital output channel (plc, dq, do, output, channel) | 10 | `.out` (0,5) | an arrowhead out of the card at (0,−8) |
| `chai` | PLC analogue input channel (plc, ai, analogue, channel) | 20 | `.+` (−5,5), `.-` (5,5) | inward arrowhead and a tilde polyline |
| `chao` | PLC analogue output channel (plc, ao, analogue, channel) | 20 | as `chai` | outward arrowhead and a tilde polyline |
| `chrtd` | PLC RTD channel (plc, rtd, temperature, channel) | 40 | `.I+` (−15,5), `.S+` (−5,5), `.S-` (5,5), `.I-` (15,5) | text `RTD` 2.5 mm |
| `chiol` | PLC IO-Link port (plc, io-link, iol, port, channel) | 30 | `.L+` (−10,5), `.C/Q` (0,5), `.L-` (10,5) | text `IOL` 2.5 mm |

Every cell: a lead from each point down to the cell's top edge at y 0; the cell box from
(−w/2, 0) to (w/2, −15), drawn with its top, bottom and both sides so that adjacent cells share an
edge; each point's role name as fixed 1.6 mm text under the lead (`+`, `S+`; none for `in`/`out`).
The pin (§4.3), the channel number, the address and the description are not graphics: they are
texts drawn from anchors (§7). Every cell point is quiet (no `Unconnected` check; a spare channel is
normal, 034 Q8).

018's `di` and `do` keep their geometry and IDs and become **compact** DI and DQ channel symbols:
the same joining rules, one point each (Q11). Nothing about them changes in a file.

### 4.2 Channel points: label = pin, key = channel and role (FP2)

Each channel point is one `Terminal` of the card device:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `label` | the **pin**: what is printed at the point and typed after the tag (`4`, `13`) |
| `key` | `.<i>.<role>`: the 0-based channel index and the role (`.3.in`, `.0.+`, `.2.C/Q`) |

The card body's own points (`L+`, `M`) keep their definition keys and labels; their keys do not
start with `.`. The channel number shown is `i + plc.channel.first` (§12, 0 by default), so
changing the first number never rewrites a terminal. Why the pin as label (Q3): the pin is what the
wire lands on, what the pack prints, what a terminal plan and a wire report name as the target, and
what 025's references show; the channel and the address are what the I/O list and the program
need, and both are derived from the key and the slot.

Old files (pre-v1, no compatibility work needed, kept because it is free): a `di`/`do` terminal
whose key does not start with `.` is read as channel `a × 8 + b` of its card from its label
`I<a>.<b>`/`Q<a>.<b>`, role `in`/`out`. Its label stays as stored.

`Kind::Plc { letter }` becomes `Kind::Channel(CardType)` in `symbols.rs` (`di` → DI, `do` → DQ);
`text_style.rs`'s `RunRule::Plc` match reads the new name (one line, lane T's file; §7.3).

### 4.3 Pins

A new channel point's pin comes from the card's **pin layout** (`plc.pins`, §12):

- `role` (built-in): pins run role by role across the card. Channel i, role r (0-based, in the
  cell's order), card of N channels: pin = r × N + i + 1. A 4-channel AI: `+` pins 1–4, `-` 5–8.
- `channel`: pins run channel by channel. Channel i, roles R: pin = i × R + r + 1. A 4-channel AI:
  channel 0 is 1, 2; channel 1 is 3, 4.

A single-role card (DI, DQ) gets pins 1…N either way. When the computed pin is already a label of
the card (the user relabelled something), the point gets the next free whole number and the echo
says so. A pin is never recomputed afterwards: changing the layout or the channel count leaves
drawn pins alone (the Renumber-style rewrite is not offered; Q4). A pin is relabelled with
`plcchannel {pins}` (§4.6).

### 4.4 Joining a card (Place Symbol on a wire or in space)

When a channel symbol of type T is placed:

1. **Device** given (prompt `Device <-A02>:` as today, offering the last card used) → it joins
   that card as **the lowest channel index not drawn yet**, or the channel named at the prompt
   `Channel <next>:` (the number shown, from `plc.channel.first`).
2. No device → a new card device of type T (prefix `A`, 018's next tag), channel 0, no rack.

Refusals, each with the way out:

- `-A02 is a DI card; pick a DI channel (chdi, di), or another card.`
- `Channel 3 of -A02 is drawn on 105-C-2. Pick another channel, or Enter for the next free one (9).`
- `-A02's 16 channels are all drawn. Raise its channel count in Properties, or pick another card.`
- On a card body that is a rack, PS, PM or CM: `-A01 is a CPU; channels go on its cards. Pick a
  card, or place a card body first.`

Ops (one transaction, labelled `Place channel`): 018's place ops; `AddTerminal { label: pin, key:
".<i>.<role>" }` per point; `AddItem`; `LinkRepresentation` with **explicit** `connection_points`
(always, also for the first symbol of a new card, as 036 §3.3). On a wire, 028's split ops for a
one-point cell; a multi-point cell on a wire lands the wire on its first point only, the others
stay open (quiet). Echo: `Placed -A02 channel 3 (pin 4) on wire 24V-12.`

### 4.5 Place Channels (`plcchannels`): the fence and the rest

```
Card [New] <-A02>:
Type [DI/DQ/AI/AO/RTD/IOL] <DI>:          (New only)
First channel <4>:                        (the lowest not drawn)
First point of the line across the wires:
Second point:                             preview: a cell, its pin and channel number at each crossing
```

- Every wire the line crosses gets the next channel's cell, in order along the line, the cell's
  first point on the wire. Wires 10 mm apart give abutting cells, so a row of DI wires becomes a
  card box. Same limits and refusals as 028's Place Terminals (most crossings: 256).
- **Rest** (`plcchannels {card, rest: true, at?}`, and the wheel row **Draw remaining channels**):
  draws every channel of the card not drawn yet as cells in a row, continuing to the right of the
  last cell of that card on the page, or at `at`. No wires. This is how spare channels are shown,
  as in the pack.
- One transaction per fence or rest. Echo: `Placed -A02 channels 0–7 on 8 wires (pins 1–8).`,
  `Drew -A02 channels 8–15 (8 spare).`

### 4.6 `plcchannel`: one channel's data

`plcchannel {card, channel, text?, address?, pins?}`, one transaction:

- `text` (1–80 characters, `null` clears): the channel's description, `plc.ch.<i>.text` on the
  card (FP3). This is the function written under the cell (`Pump 1 running`) and in the I/O list.
- `address` (1–32 characters, `null` clears): the typed address, `plc.ch.<i>.address` (FP3),
  shown as is, instead of the automatic one (§5.4).
- `pins` (`{role: label}`): relabels channel points (RemoveTerminal + AddTerminal of the same ID
  and key, 028 Q7's precedent). A pin is 1–16 characters, no spaces, unique on the card: `-A02
  already has pin 13 (channel 0, I+). Pick another label.`
- `channel` is the number shown. Echo: `-A02 channel 3: address I2.3 → "Spare sensor" … `.

`setprop` on `plc.ch.<i>.text` and `plc.ch.<i>.address` works too and goes through the same value
check (one rule, `plc::setprop_rule`, called where 034's is).

### 4.7 Inspector and page

Properties ▸ **Channel** on a channel symbol: **Channel** (read), **Pins** (one text field per role,
`ecad.props.channel.pin.<role>`), **Address** (text, placeholder the automatic address,
`ecad.props.channel.address`), **Description** (text, `ecad.props.channel.text`), and the card
section of §2.6. Hover on a cell: `-A02 channel 3 · pin 4 · I2.3 · Pump 1 running · wired to
-B05:2 (24V-12)`.

### 4.8 Wheel rows (041), appended

On a card body or channel cell: **Place channels** (`plcchannels`), **Draw remaining channels**
(`plcchannels rest`), **Slot…** (`plcslot`), **Addresses…** (`plcaddress`). On a rack body:
**Slot…** for the selected card, **Fix addresses** (`plcaddress fix`). The wheel's eight-slot rule
(041) decides their places.

## 5. Addressing (b)

### 5.1 Rule

Automatic, live, by slot and channel (Q6). For each rack, cards in slot order; one **input**
counter and one **output** counter, starting at the rack's `plc.address.start.input` and
`plc.address.start.output` (§12, 0). Each card takes a block:

| Type | Input bytes | Output bytes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DI | ⌈N / 8⌉ | 0 |
| DQ | 0 | ⌈N / 8⌉ |
| AI, RTD | N × `plc.address.analog.bytes` (2) | 0 |
| AO | 0 | N × `plc.address.analog.bytes` |
| IOL | N × `plc.address.iol.bytes` (2) | the same |
| PS, PM, CM | 0 | 0 |

N is the card's channel count (§2.3). A card's `plc.addr.size` (bytes, 1–4096) overrides the size
for its direction(s). A card's block starts at its **pinned** start `plc.addr.start` (bytes,
0–65535) when set, else at the counter; the counter then moves to the end of that block if that is
further. So a pinned card keeps its addresses when cards before it move, and the cards after it
follow it. Cards in no rack have no automatic address.

### 5.2 Formats (`plc.address.style`, rack level, §12)

| Style | Digital (channel i of a block at byte b) | Analogue, IO-Link (channel i, word at b + i × size) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `byte.bit` (built-in) | `I<b + i/8>.<i mod 8>`, `Q…` | `IW<…>`, `QW<…>` |
| `iec` | `%IX<b + i/8>.<i mod 8>`, `%QX…` | `%IW<…>`, `%QW<…>` |
| `slot` | `I:<slot>/<n:02>`, `O:<slot>/<n:02>` (n the channel number shown) | the same |
| `none` | (empty: typed addresses only) | (empty) |

An IO-Link port shows its input address; the I/O list also gives its output address (§8.1). No
vendor is named or assumed: the three styles are the generic forms question 6 named.

### 5.3 `plcaddress`

`plcaddress {rack | card, style?, input_start?, output_start?, start?, size?, fix?, clear?}`, one
transaction:

- On a rack: `style`, `input_start`, `output_start` set its object-level registry values (as
  `setdefault` does, so Settings ▸ Standards shows them, 042). `fix: true` **pins** every card in
  the rack at its current start (`plc.addr.start`), so later slot moves change no address; `clear:
  true` removes every pin (automatic again). The preview lists every address that will change.
- On a card: `start` pins it (`null` unpins), `size` overrides its size.
- Echo: `-A01: 6 cards pinned at their current addresses.`, `-A05 pinned at I8; 2 cards after it
  move (I8–I9 → I10–I11).`

### 5.4 Typed addresses

`plc.ch.<i>.address` (§4.6) wins over the automatic one everywhere (page, list, CSV, MCP), marked
`*` in tables. It is free text: no format is enforced, so any PLC's notation can be written (roadmap
question 6).

### 5.5 Limits

Counters and sums use checked arithmetic; a block that would pass 65 535 is not given an address
(the channel shows `?` in tables, a Checks row `address-overflow`); at most 999 slots and 256
channels per card are read, more is a Checks row. Never a panic.

## 6. Spare and reserved channels

Derived per channel, never stored (Q12):

| State | When |
| --- | --- |
| `used` | a point of the channel is on a net with any other connection point |
| `reserved` | not used, and the channel has a description |
| `spare` | not used, no description |

Drawn or not does not change the state; the I/O list has a Drawn column. A drawn spare cell shows
`plc.spare.text` (`Spare`, §12) where the description would be. A card's summary everywhere:
`16 channels: 9 used, 1 reserved, 6 spare`.

## 7. Text on cards and channels (with 031 and 034)

### 7.1 Channel texts (anchors appended to `symbols.rs`' `anchors`)

| Role | At (cell frame) | Align | Rotation | Text kind (031) | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pin (connection-point label) | lane T's rule at each point | — | — | connection-point label | always (031) |
| `ChannelNo` | (−w/2 + 1, −2) | left, top | 0 | connection-point label | `plc.text.channel.show` (on) |
| `Address` | (0, −12) | centre, middle | 0 | connection-point label | `plc.text.address.show` (on, Q9) |
| `ChannelText` | (0, −17) | right, middle | `plc.text.channel.rotation` (90, Q13) | description | always; `Spare` for a drawn spare |

At rotation 90 the description reads upwards and hangs below the cell, as in the pack; at 0 it is
written under the cell, centred. Anchors rotate and mirror with the symbol (034 §10). For the
compact `di`/`do`, the same roles by 034's default rule beside the box.

### 7.2 Card body caption

A new anchor role `Caption` on card bodies at (lo.x, lo.y − 2), left, top (under the body): the
registry pattern `plc.card.caption` (`{rack} slot {slot}`; `{slot:02}` pads; §12), shown when the
card is in a rack and `plc.text.caption.show` is on. So a house style that names cards by rack and
slot (`-A01 Slot02`) hides the card's tag (031's `text.tag.show` at object or project level) and
writes the caption.

### 7.3 Tags once per card

031's `text.tag.show.plc` (`first`) now means: a card's tag is shown on its body, and on a channel
cell only when the body is not on that page (once per run of cells there). The run rule's match on
`RunRule::Plc` follows the rename (§4.2); no new text entry.

## 8. The I/O list (c)

### 8.1 Rows (`iolist`, read-only, writes off allowed)

One row per channel of every card with channels, drawn or not: racks by tag, cards by slot, then
cards in no rack by tag, channels by index. `iolist {rack?, card?, state?}` returns `cards: [{card,
tag, type, rack, slot, part_number, maker, channels, used, reserved, spare, inputs, outputs}]` and
`rows`:

| Field | Kind | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rack`, `slot`, `card`, `type` | derived | §2 |
| `channel` (shown), `index` | derived | §4.2 |
| `address`, `address_out` (IO-Link), `address_typed` | **editable** / derived | `plcchannel {address}`; §5 |
| `pins: [{role, pin}]` | **editable** | `plcchannel {pins}` |
| `text` | **editable** | `plcchannel {text}` |
| `state`: `used` / `reserved` / `spare` | derived | §6 |
| `drawn`, `place` (page-column-row of the cell, 025's form), `item` | derived | the channel symbol |
| `targets: [{device, point, place}]` | derived | the other members of the nets of the channel's points, the card itself left out, nearest first (022) |
| `wire` | derived | the wire number of the first point's net (022) |
| `checks` | derived | §9 kinds on this channel |
| `editable: [field names]` | — | for 2.8's manager |

This is the row model the PLC manager (2.8) draws and edits; this design only names which fields
are editable and by which command (as 036 §9 did for terminals).

### 8.2 Generated page `iolist` ("I/O list")

A generated page kind (024's machinery, 033's continuation numbering), offered in Print Book and
the release like `terminals`:

| Slot | Card | Ch | Address | Description | Pins | Wired to | Wire | Place |

- A heading line per card: `-A01 slot 2  -A02  DI 16  <part number>  9 used, 1 reserved, 6 spare`.
- A card's rows stay on one sheet when they fit; a longer card continues with its heading
  repeated (`… continued`).
- Spare rows: `Spare` in the Description column, the rest empty; reserved rows show their
  description. Typed addresses with `*`.
- Text sizes and styles: 031's table text kinds, as the `terminals` page.

### 8.3 CSV

Release slot `io-list`, file ` I/O list.csv`, missing when the project has no card with channels
(`missing_why`: `No PLC cards with channels.`). Columns are §8.1's fields in order, one pin column
(`+:1 -:5`). The Reports export path is 027's (`outputs::csv`).

### 8.4 Lists ▸ I/O (read-only, Q14)

A column set on 039's table over `iolist` rows, grouped by card: every cell is read (grey, no box)
and goes to its source (the cell on the page, the target, the wire). Editing in the table is the
PLC manager's (2.8). It is a column set, not a panel (UI spec §5, "the rule that keeps it clean").

## 9. Checks (022 Checks; every row has a way out; nothing is refused because of a check)

| Kind | Brief | Severity | Row text | Go-to | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `slot-twice` | a | Error ● | `-A01 slot 3 holds -A04 and -A07` | the second card's body | **Move -A07 to slot 5** (next free) |
| `rack-missing` | a | Warning ▲ | `-A07 names a rack that is not in the project` | the card | **Put in rack…** (`plcslot` prompt), **Take out of rack** |
| `slot-range` | a | Warning ▲ | `-A07 is in slot 40; -A01 has slots 1–32` | the card | **Raise -A01 to 40 slots**, **Move to slot 5** |
| `unracked` | a | Info, one count row | `3 PLC cards are in no rack` | — | **Show**; per card **Put in rack…** |
| `channel-twice` | b | Error ● | `-A02 channel 3 is drawn twice (105-C-2, 106-B-4)` | the second cell | **Move to channel 9** (next not drawn) |
| `channel-over` | b | Warning ▲ | `-A02 has channel 17 drawn; it has 16 channels` | the cell | **Raise to 17 channels** |
| `channel-type` | b | Warning ▲ | `-A02 is a DI card; channel 4 is drawn as AI` | the cell | **Move to a new AI card** |
| `address-overlap` | b | Warning ▲ | `-A05 inputs I2–I3 overlap -A04 inputs I2–I3 (-A01)` | the later card | **Move -A05 after -A04** (pins it at the next free byte), **Unpin -A05** |

`address-twice` compares the channels of one rack (racks are separate stations; Q8 notes the

## 10. Fit with 018, 022, 024, 025, 026, 028/036, 031, 034, 020, 039

### 10.1 018 / 034

`Kind::Channel` replaces `Kind::Plc` (§4.2); `di`/`do` keep their definitions. 034's card bodies
become cards with no definition change, as 034 §6 promised; a project with bodies placed before this
brief has cards in no rack (`unracked` Info row, fix **Put in rack…**).

### 10.2 024 BOM

Racks and cards are devices: one BOM row each from their `part_number`/`maker`, as today. Nothing
new. A CPU's memory card or a card's front connector as assembly rows are 1.12's (047).

### 10.3 025 references

Channel symbols are children of the card for 025's index: a cell on a page without the card body
shows the body's place (`/105.C4`) under its channel text, as a contact shows its coil's. The body's
channel table (`ch  address  place`, 025 Q10) is drawn only when `plc.xref.table` is on (§12, off:
the pack does not draw one). No index change.

### 10.4 026 numbering

Renumber of devices covers racks and cards (they are devices). Pins are never renumbered (a
`Kind::Channel` terminal is not a strip terminal). `plc.rack` holds the ID, so renumbering a rack
moves nothing.

### 10.5 028 / 036 terminal plans and wire reports

A strip terminal wired to a channel names it as `-A02:4` (tag and pin), with the card's caption in
the hover (`-A01 slot 2`). A target style that writes the rack and slot instead (`-A01 Slot02:4`)
is not built; Q16.

### 10.6 020 merge

All new data are properties: merged key by key. Two branches putting two cards in one slot, or
drawing one channel twice, merge cleanly and show `slot-twice` / `channel-twice`.

### 10.7 039 / 042 / 041

Lists ▸ I/O is a column set (§8.4); the inspector fields of §2.6 and §4.7 and the wheel rows of
§4.8 go into 042's and 041's tables. No panel, dialog or toolbar.

## 11. Update maps and cost

### 11.1 The `Plc` model (kept, like 028's `Strips`)

Per rack: slots → cards, address blocks. Per card: type, channel count, channels (index → points,
cells, state, target, wire), block. Indexes: `by_rack`, `card_of_terminal`, `card_of_rep`,
`channel_of_net` (net → channel points on it), `by_target` (device → rows whose targets include
it), `by_page` (page → cells on it), `inherits` (registry key → racks or cards that read it from the
project or built-in). Every op not listed does nothing to the PLC model:

| Op (kind, target) | Recomputed |
| --- | --- |
| `CreateComponent` / `DeleteComponent` of a rack or card | that card or rack; its rack's blocks from its slot on |
| `SetProperty(card, plc.rack \| plc.slot)` | the old and new rack's slot maps; their blocks from the lower of the old and new slot on; address texts on `by_page` of the cards whose block moved |
| `SetProperty(card, plc.channels \| plc.addr.start \| plc.addr.size)` | that card; its rack's blocks after it; their address texts |
| `SetProperty(card, plc.ch.<i>.text \| plc.ch.<i>.address)` | that channel's row; the text on the page showing its cell |
| `SetProperty(rack, plc.address.* \| plc.slots \| plc.slot.first)` and other `plc.*` registry keys at object level | that rack's cards; their texts |
| `SetProperty(Project, plc.*)` / `setdefault` at project level | racks and cards in `inherits[key]` |
| `AddTerminal` / `RemoveTerminal` of a card | that card's channels |
| `LinkRepresentation` / `UnlinkRepresentation` / `ModifyItem` of a card's symbol | that card's drawn cells, places, `channel-twice`, `channel-type` |
| a net change (022's touched nets) on `channel_of_net` | those channels' state, targets, wire |
| a page renumber, or a `ModifyItem` moving a cell | `place` of the cells on that page |
| `SetProperty(device, tag)` | rows in `by_target[device]`; if a rack or card, its rows and its cards' captions |
| anything else | nothing |

The I/O page, Lists ▸ I/O and `iolist` callers redraw only when `Plc::last_changed` is not empty.
A slot move can shift the addresses of every later card in that rack: the cost is bounded by one
rack (at most 999 slots × 256 channels) and only the pages showing those cells redraw.

### 11.2 Bench (`cargo xtask bench`, large tier at the end of each brief; medium while building)

The large bench project gains 10 racks × 12 cards (DI 16, DQ 16, AI 4, RTD 4, IOL 4), channels
drawn by fences across 30 pages, 70 % used, descriptions on all used channels.

| Row | Measures | Target | Brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `plcbuild ms` | first build of the `Plc` model | < 30 ms | a |
| `slotmove ms` | move a card from slot 2 to 12 (10 cards' addresses and texts move), commit, update, redraw | < 16 ms | b |
| `chfence ms` | fence 16 channels, commit, update | < 16 ms | b |
| `plcstyle ms` | change one rack's address style, commit, update, redraw | < 16 ms | b |
| `iolist ms` | first `iolist` of all rows; one channel text edit, update | < 50 ms; < 5 ms | c |

`netupd`, `stripupd`, `xref` and `draw_window` must not get worse by more than 10 %. Verify mode
compares the kept and rebuilt `Plc` model and asserts no ordinary edit rebuilds it.

## 14. Format proposals (for the overseer)

Pre-v1: no compatibility work is needed. No new op and no new record field; everything is
properties, terminal fields that exist, and code.

- **FP1. Card properties `plc.rack` and `plc.slot`.** `plc.rack` holds another component's ID in
  its 32-hex text form. This is **the first property that refers to a component by ID**: merge,
  diff and the inspector show it as the rack's tag; deleting a rack removes the keys from its cards in
  the same transaction (§2.2, Q1); a dangling ID that still arrives (a hand-made file, a merge
  where one branch deleted the rack) is a Checks row (§9).
- **FP2. Channel point keys** `.<i>.<role>` in the existing `Terminal.key`; the label is the pin.
  Old `di`/`do` keys (`I0.0`) read as channel `a × 8 + b` (§4.2).
- **FP3. Card properties** `plc.channels`, `plc.addr.start`, `plc.addr.size`, `plc.ch.<i>.text`,
  `plc.ch.<i>.address` (i the 0-based index).
- **FP4. Registry keys stored as object properties** on racks and cards (§12), as 031's entries
  are; no new mechanism.
- **FP5. Page property `generated = iolist`**: a new value of the existing key (024).
- **FP6 (ops recorded, not format).** `plcslot` (swap), `plcaddress fix/clear`, the fence and rest
  transactions, `plcchannel {pins}` (RemoveTerminal + AddTerminal of the same ID and key).
- **FP7 (code, listed because it changes shared meaning).** `Kind::Plc { letter }` becomes
  `Kind::Channel(CardType)`: `di`/`do` join a card as its next free channel with the pin as label,
  not the next address. The registry gains the `plc` area. Nothing in `ecad-model` changes.

**Code, not format:** the `plc` module; the new bodies and cells; `plcslot`, `plcracks`,
`plcchannels`, `plcchannel`, `plcaddress`, `iolist`; Checks kinds; the `iolist` page and CSV;
`defaults/plc.rs`; Lists ▸ I/O.

**Decision (overseer, 2026-11-13):** FP1–FP7 are approved.

- **FP1** is approved on four conditions, which are acceptance criteria of 045a:
  1. merge, diff and the inspector show the rack's **tag**, never the hex ID;
  2. deleting a rack clears `plc.rack` and `plc.slot` on its cards in the same transaction;
  3. a dangling ID is a Checks row (`rack-missing`), never a crash;
  4. a round-trip test (save, reopen, compare) covers the reference.
- **FP2:** approved. Reading old `di`/`do` keys (`I<a>.<b>`) as channel `a × 8 + b` keeps today's
  symbols working; it is not compatibility work.
- **FP7:** approved. The `plc` registry area joins 047 FP6's one-line-per-area `all()` list;
  whichever of 045 and 047 lands first makes that change.

## 15. Owner questions, each with a recommendation

**Answered (owner, 2026-11-13): all seventeen "as recommended".** The recommended option of each
question below is the decision.

1. **A card is its own device with its own tag** (`-A02`), in a rack by a property that holds the
   rack's ID; its caption shows `-A01 slot 2`; **deleting a rack takes its cards out of it** in the
   same transaction (the cards stay). **Recommend yes**: cards are bought, listed and referenced
   like any device, and retagging a rack touches nothing.
2. **The rack is the CPU or a head module; a remote I/O station is a rack of its own; racks do not
   nest.** **Recommend yes.**
3. **A channel point's label is its pin** (`-A02:4`), with the channel and role in its key and the
   channel number and address derived. **Recommend yes**: the pin is what the wire lands on and
   what the pack prints; addresses and numbering can then change without rewriting a terminal.
4. **Pin layout `role` by default** (all `+` pins, then all `-`), `channel` as the alternative,
   both per project or card; pins are filled once when drawn and never rewritten. **Recommend
   yes.**
5. **Generic channel roles only** (DI `in`; DQ `out`; AI/AO `+`/`-`; RTD `I+ S+ S- I-`; IO-Link
   `L+ C/Q L-`); vendor-shaped channels (for example voltage and current points on one channel)
   come from the symbol builder (1.1). **Recommend yes**: no vendor is built in (question 6).
6. **Addresses are live**, derived from slot and channel, with a **pinned start per card** and a
   **typed address per channel**; `plcaddress fix` pins a whole rack. This refines decided
   question 6, which spoke of a command that fills addresses: a stored copy would go stale when a
   card moves, which is the "update/retag" button the roadmap avoids. **Recommend yes.**
7. **Address sizes:** digital ⌈N/8⌉ bytes, analogue 2 bytes per channel, IO-Link 2 bytes per port
   in and out, one input and one output counter per rack, a per-card size override. **Recommend
   yes**; tell us a card your panels use that does not fit.
8. **Styles** `byte.bit` (`I0.0`, built-in), `iec` (`%IX0.0`), `slot` (`I:3/04`), `none`;
   duplicate addresses checked within a rack only. **Recommend yes.**
9. **Addresses shown on the channel by default** (the roadmap asks for them; the pack shows pins
   only, and its house style would switch them off). **Recommend on.**
10. **Channel cells, a fence and "Draw remaining channels"** rather than one whole-card symbol per
    channel count. **Recommend yes**: one cell per type covers every card size, and a card can be
    drawn in parts on several pages.
11. **`di` and `do` stay**, unchanged in shape, as compact DI/DQ channel symbols that join cards.
    **Recommend yes**: existing drawings keep working, and a single channel in a circuit reads
    well compact.
12. **Spare and reserved are derived** (no wire; reserved when described), and a drawn spare
    prints `Spare`. **Recommend yes.**
13. **Channel descriptions rotated 90° by default**, horizontal as a house-style choice.
    **Recommend yes.**
14. **Lists ▸ I/O as a read-only column set now**; editing in the table is the PLC manager (2.8).
    **Recommend yes**: it is a column set on the existing table, not a panel.
15. **The rack overview** (UI spec screen m) and **its generated sheet wait for 2.8**; `plcracks`
    gives their data now. **Recommend yes.**
16. **References name a card by tag and pin** (`-A02:4`), not rack and slot (`-A01 Slot02:4`); a
    rack-slot reference style only if you miss it after using this. **Recommend yes.**
17. **Placing a card body asks Rack and Slot** when the project has a rack (Enter takes the next
    free slot). **Recommend yes.**

## 16. Out of scope

The PLC manager's editing and the rack overview (2.8); importing or exporting assignment lists and
PLC-tool exchange (2.5, after v1); vendor channel shapes and pin maps (1.1, 2.3 parts); a channel's

channel count; nested racks and extension racks with their own numbering; addresses checked across
racks; a rack-slot reference style (Q16); PLC terminals as strip positions; assembly rows such as
memory cards (1.12).
