# Design 022: Connectivity and wire numbers

Brief: `docs/briefs/022-connectivity-and-wire-numbers.md` (Approved 2026-11-08), feature F4,
branch `feature/connectivity`. Builds on 008 (wires and connection points), 010 (cable references),
016 (Project panel, Devices tab), 018 (devices, starter symbols, `place`, `setprop`) and 019's
Properties panel (`origin/feature/sheets`, §8). Words: `docs/glossary.md`; four new words are
proposed in §1. Tokens: `docs/design/tokens.md` (§12 adds five). Owner questions: §16.

## 0. What the model has today (checked)

- A **conductor** has two ends, each attached to one connection point or loose (`Conductor.ends`,
  set by `CreateConductor` and `AttachEnd`). Connectivity is attachments only: geometry is never
  consulted (DOC-03). `derive::nets` already unions conductors and connection points, but over the
  whole project on every call.
- The `wire` command attaches an end that lands on a connection point; an end landing on the
  middle of another wire stays loose. There is no way to tap a wire mid-route: a T needs a
  connection point there.
- **Cable cores** are ordinary conductors (`CableMember.conductor`), and a cable continued on
  another page (010) is the *same* conductors with a second route. Nets therefore already pass
  through cores and cable references with no extra rule.
- There is **no** cross-page link for a single wire (a 24 V rail on three pages); the demo's 24 V
  and 0 V wires end loose at the page edge.
- A starter **terminal** (`-X01:1`) has one connection point: the field wire and the panel wire
  land on the same point, so they are one net without any bridge. No starter symbol has an
  internal bridge; `Definition` cannot express one.
- `SetProperty` targets components, conductors and cables (019 adds pages and the project).
  `ProjectSettings` is `#[serde(default)]` and changed by `SetProjectSettings`.

## 1. Goal and words

ECAD knows what is connected: nets are derived live from what is drawn, every conductor shows its
net's wire number with its cross-section and colour (`24V-07 0.5 Blue`), adding a wire never
renumbers other nets, and a Checks list finds open ends and false-looking connections.

Proposed glossary rows (Q12):

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **junction** | The dot where three or more wires meet away from a device: a connection point of its own. A T without a junction is not connected. |
| **continuation** | An arrow with a name (`24V`) where a net leaves a page. All continuations with one name are one net. |
| **potential** | What a wire carries for numbering and defaults: `L`, `L1`…`L3`, `N`, `PE`, `24V`, `0V`. It sets the number prefix, colour and cross-section. Several nets share one potential (`24V-01`, `24V-02`). |
| **wire number** | The number a net carries and every one of its conductors shows. Automatic, or **fixed** by hand. |

## 2. Net derivation rules

A **net** is a connected set of connection points and conductors, joined by:
1. a conductor end attached to a connection point (`AttachEnd`, `CreateConductor`);
2. two or more ends on one connection point (they are all in its net);
3. **continuations with the same name** (trimmed, compared case-insensitively): their connection
   points are one net, on any page;
4. **device-internal bridges** from the definition, only if FP5 is approved (§13).

Nothing else joins. In particular:
- **Crossings never connect.** Two routes crossing are two nets whatever the drawing shows.
- **A T needs a junction.** A wire end lying on the middle of another wire is loose (Checks:
  "looks connected", §8). A junction is a tagless component with one connection point, drawn as a
  filled dot (§3.1). Its three or more wires attach to that point.
- A conductor with no ends attached is a net of its own (one conductor, no connection points).
- A connection point with nothing attached is a net of its own with no conductor. It has no wire
  number (numbers live on conductors) and is not listed by `nets` unless asked (`all: true`).
- Cable cores and cable references need no rule (same conductor, §0). A wire attached to a
  terminal (`-X02:1`) and the cable core on the same point are one net: the number runs through.

## 3. Drawing junctions and continuations

### 3.1 Junction

**Model (existing ops only, one transaction, label `Junction`):** for a wire C clicked at point P
(snapped to the nearest route vertex or the 5 mm grid point on the route):
1. `CreateComponent { properties: {kind: "junction"} }`, `EmbedDefinition` of the starter
   `junction` (first use only), `AddTerminal` (key `1`), `AddItem` (symbol instance at P, C's
   layer) and `LinkRepresentation` (Symbol form).
2. Split C at P: `ModifyItem` shortens C's route to end at P; `AttachEnd { C, B, before: old,
   after: J }`; `CreateConductor` C2 with **a copy of C's properties** (number, potential,
   cross-section, colour), `AddItem` + `LinkRepresentation` for C2's route from P, `AttachEnd
   { C2, A, J }` and `AttachEnd { C2, B, old }`.
3. Any loose wire end lying at P (within `ecad.aperture`) is attached to J: `AttachEnd`.

C keeps its ID, so its other page references and history stay. Refused, nothing written:
- `-W01's core 2 can't take a junction: a cable core runs end to end.` (C is a cable member);
- `This wire is drawn on 2 pages; add the junction where it is a single wire.` (C has routes on
  other pages, only possible for cores today);
- `There is a junction here already.`;
- `Click on a wire, not its end: an end can attach to a connection point.` (P is a route end).

**Look:** starter `junction`: a circle of radius 0.5 mm (`ecad.junction-size`) drawn with
lineweight 0.50 mm so it plots and shows filled with `LWDISPLAY` on; if it does not read as
filled in the screenshot, three concentric circles (0.5, 0.33, 0.17 mm). No tag, no label.
Junctions and continuations are **not** listed in the Devices tab (filter `kind`), nor counted in
"No tag".

**Auto-junction in the Wire tool (Q3, recommended):** a wire end placed on the interior of
another wire (aperture snap shows a `ecad.attach-preview` ring with the text `junction`) adds the
junction in the same transaction as the wire. The JSON `wire` has `junction: true` by default, so
a script behaves as clicks do; `false` leaves the end loose. Erasing a junction is erasing a
device: its ends become loose (008 behaviour), the halves are not rejoined.

### 3.2 Continuation

Starter `continuation`: connection point `1` at (0, 0); an arrow from (0, 0) to (0, −5) with a
head (−1.5, −3.5)–(0, −5)–(1.5, −3.5), pointing away from its wire; rotation 0/90/180/270 as any
symbol. Component properties `kind: "continuation"` and `name` (`24V`). Placed with Find Symbol
(`continuation`, aliases `arrow`, `potential`, `link`), which asks `Name <24V>:` (the last name
used; first time empty) before the click; `place {symbol: "continuation", at, rotation, name}`.

Drawn beside it (derived, not stored, like 018 tags): the **name** at local (2.5, −4), height
`ecad.tag-height`, `ecad.tag`; under it the **other places** with the same name, in book order,
as reference texts in `ecad.reference` at `ecad.ref-text-height`: `/2.1 /3.1` (010 form; 019's
form `105-C-4` once rebased). Above three: `/2.1 /3.1 /4.2 +3`. With no partner nothing is drawn
under the name (a single named flag is legitimate); Checks lists it as Info.

Name rules: trimmed, 1–16 characters, letters, digits and `+ - / . _`; else refused
`A continuation name is 1 to 16 letters, digits or + - / . _`. Renaming (`setprop` `name`, or the
Properties panel) moves its net to the new name group in one transaction.

## 4. The incremental algorithm

**Where:** `ecad-model`, a derived `Connectivity` (not serialised, not compared), next to the
reverse indexes. It is built once on open, reload, and after a ledger replay, and then updated
from each committed transaction, undo and redo from its ops (`Touched` plus the ops' before and
after values). Nothing scans the project per edit.

**Structure.** Nodes: connection points, conductors and continuation name groups. `net_of: Node →
NetIx`; per net: its terminals, conductors and names, and counted multisets of its conductors'
`wire_number`, `wire_number_fixed`, `potential`. An index `number → nets` and per numbering scope
`n → count` (§5.3). A name index `name key → continuation components` joins the `Index` (like
tags).

**Updates.**
- **Join** (end attached, conductor created with ends, continuation added or renamed into a
  group, bridge added): union by size: the smaller net's members are relabelled into the larger.
  Amortised O(log n) per node.
- **Cut** (end detached or re-attached, conductor deleted, connection point removed, continuation
  removed or renamed out, bridge removed): the net is marked suspect with the cut's two sides.
  After the transaction's ops, for each suspect net run two breadth-first searches from the two
  sides, one step each in turn, using the document's indexes (`ends_at`, `Conductor.ends`, the
  name index). If they meet, nothing changes; if one side runs out first, that side is the new
  net. Cost: at most twice the smaller part when it splits, the net's size otherwise. Never the
  project.
- Moves, geometry, pages, layers, cables and every op not in §11's map: nothing.

**Targets (bench, `cargo xtask bench`, large tier: 500 pages, 25,000 wires):**

| Column | What | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nets ms` | full build on open | ≤ 100 ms |
| `netupd ms` | one `wire` attached at both ends, then undone: nets, numbers, labels on screen, checks | ≈ 1 ms (≤ 2 ms) |
| `netcut ms` | delete one conductor of the largest net (the 0V net) | ≤ 5 ms |

A test applies a long random op sequence and compares `Connectivity` with a fresh build after
each step (as `check_index` does for the indexes).

## 5. Wire numbers

### 5.1 Where a number is kept (FP2, Q1)

The net carries the number; it is stored on **every conductor of the net** as conductor property
`wire_number` (invariant W: after every commit, all conductors of a net carry the same
`wire_number`). A fixed number also sets `wire_number_fixed = "yes"` on them. The net's number is
read from its conductors, so a conductor deleted from a net never loses it, and undo restores
numbers exactly because they are recorded `SetProperty` ops.

**The numbering pass** runs inside every ECAD commit (`apply_edit`, the translate observer's
commits, MCP `execute`) after the command's own ops and before the transaction is closed, on the
nets the transaction touched only, and appends `SetProperty` ops to the **same** transaction (one
`u` undoes the wire and its number). It never runs on undo, redo, replay, open or with writes off.
How the pass sees the post-op state is the worker's choice, but it must not clone the document.
It is a pure function in `ecad-model` (`wiring::assign(doc, connectivity, touched) → Vec<Op>`).

### 5.2 Rules (applied by the pass, per touched net, in conductor-ID order)

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| New net (no conductor carries a number) | Next free number of its scope (§5.3), written on its conductors |
| Conductor added to a numbered net | It gets the net's number (one `SetProperty`) |
| Two nets merge, both automatic | The number carried by **more conductors** wins; a tie goes to the lower `n`. The others are rewritten |
| Merge, one fixed | The fixed number wins and is written (fixed) on all |
| Merge, two different fixed | No rewrite. Checks: error "one net, two fixed numbers"; labels show the number of the lowest conductor ID in `ecad.orphan` |
| A net splits, automatic | The part with more conductors keeps the number (tie: the part with the lowest conductor ID); the other gets the next free |
| A net splits, fixed | Both keep it. Checks: error "two nets share 24V-07" |
| Its potential changes (§6) and the number is automatic with another prefix | Renumbered to the next free of the new potential (Q7). A fixed number stays |
| Scheme, digits or prefixes change in settings | Nothing; existing numbers stay until **Renumber Wires** |
| Conductor deleted, moved, restyled | Nothing for the others |

**Fixing a number** (`wirenumber`): writes `wire_number` and `wire_number_fixed` on all the net's
conductors in one transaction. Value rules: trimmed, 1–24 characters, no spaces or control
characters; refused when another net carries it: `24V-07 is used by another net (-K01:A1,
/2.1). Choose another.` **Releasing** (`value: null`) removes `wire_number_fixed` and keeps the
value as an automatic number. **Renumber Wires** (`renumberwires`) is the only command that
changes automatic numbers wholesale: scope `unnumbered` (default) or `all` (automatic ones only,
in book order: page, then route start top-to-bottom, left-to-right), one transaction.

Projects made before 022 open with no numbers (opening never writes): labels show `?` and Checks
offers **Number them** (`renumberwires {scope: "unnumbered"}`). A net touched by an edit is
numbered by the pass anyway.

### 5.3 Schemes and the next free number

Project setting `scheme` (FP1). `{p}` is the potential's prefix, `{n}` the sequence padded to
`digits` (default 2), `{page}` the page number (019; its position until then), `{col}` the column.

| Scheme | Number | Sequence scope | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `potential` (default) | `{p}{n}` | per prefix, project-wide | `24V-07`, `L-01` |
| `page` | `{p}{page}.{n}` | per prefix and page | `24V-105.03` |
| `page-column` | `{p}{page}{col}.{k}` (k unpadded) | per prefix, page and column | `105C.1` |

Page and column are those of the net's lowest-ID conductor's route start on its first page in
book order, **when the number is assigned**; moving the wire later does not renumber it.
**Next free** is the highest `n` used in the scope plus one (fixed numbers that match the pattern
count as used). Gaps are never filled (Q6). A potential marked `numbered: false` (default: `PE`)
gives every net the prefix alone (`PE`), shared and exempt from the duplicate check.

## 6. Potentials, colour and cross-section

A net's **potential**, first match wins:
1. the `potential` property its conductors carry (the most common value; a second distinct value
   is a Checks warning "mixed potentials");
2. the name of a continuation in it that matches a potential in the settings table;
3. none: prefix `unclassified_prefix` (default empty, so `07`; Q8).

Two continuations with different names in one net, or a conductor potential that disagrees with a
continuation name in the table, is a Checks **error** "net joins 24V and 0V".

**The Wire tool's potential.** The `wire` prompt gains a keyword: `Wire first point or
[Potential] <auto>:`; `P` asks `Potential for new wires [auto/L/N/PE/24V/0V/…] <auto>:` from the
settings table (any table name; `auto` writes nothing and the wire takes its net's potential).
The choice stays for the session and is shown in the prompt (`<24V>`). JSON: `wire {…,
potential: "24V"}` writes `potential` on the new conductor in the same transaction.
**Set Potential** for a whole net is the Properties panel's Potential field (§9) or `potential
{conductor, value}`: it writes `potential` on all the net's conductors in one transaction.

**Cross-section and colour** are conductor properties `cross_section` and `colour`, free text
(trimmed, ≤ 24 characters, `18 AWG` allowed). Unset, the conductor shows its potential's default
from the settings table (derived, not written); changing a default updates every conductor that
has none. Never enforced: any colour on any potential is accepted and no check compares them.

Default settings table (IEC 60445 and 60204-1 practice, Q10):

| Potential | Prefix | Numbered | Colour | Cross-section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `L` | `L-` | yes | Brown | 2.5 |
| `L1` / `L2` / `L3` | `L1-` / `L2-` / `L3-` | yes | Brown / Black / Grey | 2.5 |
| `N` | `N-` | yes | Blue | 2.5 |
| `PE` | `PE` | no | Green-Yellow | 2.5 |
| `24V` | `24V-` | yes | Blue | 0.5 |
| `0V` | `0V-` | yes | Blue | 0.5 |

A potential not in the table but named by a continuation gets prefix `<name>-` and no defaults.

## 7. Conductor labels

**Text:** `<number> <cross-section> <colour>`, single spaces, missing parts left out (`24V-07
Blue`); `?` for a net with no number yet. Cable cores show the number only: their colour and size
are the cable's (009 core labels, F5).

**Where:** one label per route per page (a conductor continued on two pages is labelled on both),
on the route's **longest segment** (first if equal), centred on its midpoint, offset
`ecad.label-gap` from the line:
- horizontal: above the line, reading left to right;
- vertical: left of the line, rotated 90°, reading bottom to top;
- oblique: along the segment, above it, the angle normalised into (−90°, 90°] so the text is
  never upside down.

Height `ecad.label-height` (2.5 mm), colour `ecad.wire-label`, on the reserved layer
`ECAD-WIRE-LABELS` (projected like 009's `ECAD-CABLES`: derived, not stored, plotted). A label
whose net is in a number error (§8) is drawn in `ecad.orphan`. A label longer than its segment is
drawn anyway. No collision avoidance and no dragging in v0.1 (Q11). Clicking a label selects its
wire; hovering it shows the wire's hover lens with a new line: `Net 24V-04 · 3 wires · -S02:14,
-K01:14, -K01:A1, -H01:X1` (first 6 points, then `+n`).

## 8. Checks

**Where:** a third tab in the Project panel, **Checks**, beside Pages and Devices (016 §16),
titled `Checks (5)` with the count of shown entries. Live: no refresh button.

```
│ [Pages] [Devices] [Checks (5)]               │
│ Show: [All kinds ▾]   2 errors · 3 warnings  │
│ ● Net joins 24V and 0V        /2.1  24V-01   │  severity glyph, what, where, which
│ ▲ Open end                    /3.2  -W01 3   │
│ ▲ Looks connected, no junction /2.3 0V-01    │
│ · Connection point not connected /3.4 -M01:PE│
│ · Continuation without partner /1.1  24V     │
```

| Kind | Severity | Entry, where it points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net joins two potentials | Error ● | the continuation or conductor that disagrees |
| Two nets share a number | Error ● | each net's first conductor (one row per net) |
| One net, two fixed numbers | Error ● | the first conductor of each fixed value |
| Open end (one end loose) | Warning ▲ | the loose end |
| Loose wire (both ends loose) | Warning ▲ | its route midpoint |
| Looks connected, no junction: a loose end lying on another wire's interior, or two loose ends at one point | Warning ▲ | the point; the row has **Add junction** (`junction {at}`) |
| End drawn away from its connection point (`derive::mismatches`) | Warning ▲ | the end |
| Net with a single connection point (conductors, one point, no continuation) | Warning ▲ | the point |
| Junction with fewer than three wires | Info · | the junction |
| Connection point not connected (device points only; not junctions or continuations) | Info · | the point, `-M01:PE` |
| Continuation without partner | Info · | the continuation |
| Mixed potentials in one net | Info · | the first disagreeing conductor |
| Unnumbered nets (projects older than 022) | Info · | one row: `12 nets have no number` with **Number them** |

Glyph colours: error `ecad.check-error`, warning `ecad.check-warning`, info `cad.text_dim`. Rows
sorted by severity, then book order, then position. **Show** lists the kinds with check boxes
(all on; kept for the session). Empty: `No problems found.` Read-only project: the same, without
the fix buttons.

**Go to:** clicking a row makes its page the active page, centres the view on the point at the
current zoom (kept between 60 and 600 mm high, as 016 §16), selects the object, and draws an
`ecad.preview-ring` in the row's severity colour on the point until the next click or Esc.
Nothing is recorded. ↑/↓ move between rows, Enter goes to.

## 13. Format and ops proposals (the overseer decides)

**No new op is needed.** Everything is existing ops plus `SetProperty` and `SetProjectSettings`.

- **FP1. Wiring settings (required, additive).** `ProjectSettings.wiring: WiringSettings {
  scheme, digits, unclassified_prefix, potentials: Vec<{name, prefix, numbered, colour,
  cross_section}> }`, `#[serde(default)]` and omitted at its default, so old files open with the
  §6 table and an old file saved again is unchanged; `ecad-file`'s `v1` mirror gains it. Changed by
  `SetProjectSettings`. *Alternative:* 019's FP1 project properties (`wiring.scheme`,
  `wiring.potential.<n>.colour`…): no type, and it depends on 019 merging first.
- **FP2. Automatic numbers are stored (policy, required).** Derived state is never stored
  (EEP-0002), but a wire number is printed on a physical marker: once issued it is identity, and
  no derivation from the drawing alone can keep it when nets are deleted, merged or split.
  Options: **A (recommended)** the numbering pass writes `wire_number` on the net's conductors in
  the user's own transaction (§5.1); **B** a **Number Wires** command only (common in other tools):
  new nets show `?` until it runs, so AC2 holds only after it; **C** numbers derived from net
  order: rejected, a deletion or split renumbers later nets.
- **FP3. Property keys (no format change).** Conductor: `wire_number`, `wire_number_fixed`,
  `potential`, `cross_section`, `colour`. Component: `kind` (`junction`, `continuation`), `name`.
- **FP4. Junctions and continuations are tagless components** with starter definitions
  `junction` and `continuation`. *Rejected:* a new `Junction` object and ops: a connection point
  already has to belong to a component, and attaching, moving, erasing and undo come for free.
- **FP5. Device-internal bridges.** Options: **(a, recommended)** `Definition.bridges:
  Vec<Vec<String>>` (groups of terminal keys joined inside), omitted when empty so existing
  definitions keep their content hashes; **(b)** a component property `bridges` (`1=2;3=4`),
  editable per device, no format change, but not "from the definition"; **(c)** defer to F5. No
  starter symbol needs one in v0.1 (the terminal has one point), so (a) ships the rule and a test
  definition only.

## 16. Owner questions, each with a recommendation

1. **Stored automatic numbers (FP2).** *Recommend A:* written by the pass in the same transaction,
   so numbers are live and stable; B is the fallback if stored derived state is unacceptable.
2. **Potential is a per-wire choice (voltage class), set by the Wire tool, Properties, or a
   continuation's name**, so switched control wires stay `24V-nn`. *Recommend yes.*
3. **Auto-junction** when a wire ends on another wire's interior. *Recommend yes* (Wire tool and
   JSON default); a separate **Junction** tool remains for existing Ts.
4. **Continuations in 022.** Without them a potential cannot cross pages except through cables.
   *Recommend yes*, minimal: place, name, other places, go to.
5. **Merge winner:** the number on more conductors, ties to the lower number. *Recommend yes.*
6. **Next free = highest + 1**, gaps never refilled (a deleted top number can return). *Recommend
   yes;* the alternative "never reuse" needs a stored counter.
7. **A potential change renumbers that net's automatic number** to the new prefix. *Recommend yes;*
   fixed numbers never change.
8. **Nets with no potential** numbered `07` with no prefix. *Recommend yes;* or a prefix such as
   `W-`.
9. **Properties panel rows wait for 019's rebase**; until then hover and commands. *Recommend
   yes.*
10. **Default table (§6)**: N `Blue` (IEC says light blue), 0V `Blue` like 24V, `PE` unnumbered.
    *Recommend as shown;* your practice may differ.
11. **Labels:** one per route per page, longest segment, no dragging in v0.1. *Recommend yes;*
    a stored label offset is later work.
12. **Glossary:** add junction, continuation, potential, wire number (§1). *Recommend yes.*
13. **Bridges (FP5).** *Recommend (a)*, with no starter using it until F5.
14. **Duplicate numbers are refused when typed**; only a split of a fixed net can make one (a
    Checks error). *Recommend yes.*

## 17. Out of scope

Terminal strips, terminal plans and jumpers between terminals (F5); wire, cable and terminal lists
and CSV (F6); per-conductor (rather than per-net) numbering; label dragging and collision
avoidance; PLC I/O lists; colour or cross-section rules and checks; wire numbers on cable core
labels in fan view (009 keeps its core labels); continuations by page-edge position (only names).
