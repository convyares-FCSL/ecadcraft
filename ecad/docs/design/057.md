# Design 057: wire and cable rating checks

Status: Draft (designer, 2026-11-14). Questions in §14, format proposals in §13.
Brief: `docs/briefs/057-ratings.md` (Draft). Release **0.2.2**, UI rev 4.
Source: the rev 4 build spec (14 November 2026), sections "Wires, with conductor rating", "Cables,
with IEC rating" and "Calculations: where each one lives", and boards **2.05** (Project ▸ Wires,
with conductor rating) and **2.06** (Project ▸ Cables). Rev 4's rule: **calculations are checks,
not separate tools.** A result shows where the item is, the basis it used, and a fix.

Builds on 022 (potentials, conductor `colour` and `cross_section`, the Checks tab), 028 (cables,
core offers, `type` notation), 034 (the device `rating` property, `rating.<n>` on fuse banks), 036
(fuse positions on strips), 047 (part lines; the part set is not used here, §4.4), 050 (the style
model, wire categories, the reserved catalogue word `cable`, FP7) and rev 3's Project ▸ Wires and
Cables tabs. Follows `008.md` §2: one commit is one JSON-equivalent transaction, Esc writes nothing,
feedback in words, never crash.

**Safety.** These numbers decide whether a conductor is big enough. Every built-in table value is
transcribed by a person from a licensed copy of the standard and checked by a second person
(§5.4). Until a table is checked, its results carry "unverified" and one Info row says so. The
example values in this document are **test fixtures**, not the standard's values, unless a row
says otherwise. The designer worked from public descriptions of the methods only.

## 1. Goal and words

**Goal:** the user sees, without opening a calculator, whether each panel wire and each field cable
is big enough for the device that protects it, and whether a cable's volt drop is within its
limit, with the numbers that led there and a way to fix it.

- **Iz**: what the conductor can carry continuously where it is installed (**Carries** on the
  boards).
- **In**: the protective device's rated current (`-Q01 4 A`).
- **Ib**: the design (load) current. Typed now; supplied by the Power brief (0.2.3) later.
- **Basis**: how Iz is found. **Datasheet** (the part's rating × factors), **Table** (IEC 60364-5-52
  current-carrying capacity × factors; the cable boards say "IEC table") or **Manual** (typed Iz).
- **Rated subject**: a conductor (panel wire) or a cable (field cable).

The checks are the overload condition of IEC 60364-4-43 / BS 7671 433.1.1: **Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz**. The
second condition (I2 ≤ 1.45 Iz) holds for circuit-breakers to IEC 60898 / IEC 60947-2 when In ≤ Iz,
and for gG fuses to IEC 60269 by the same margin. 057 states this in the result tooltip and does
not compute I2. **Fault protection (k²S², Zs, disconnection times) is out of scope** (Q13).

## 3. Calculations

All arithmetic is `f64` on parsed, bounded inputs (§6). A non-finite intermediate result is
treated as "no result" with the Info row `rating-no-data`, never shown or compared.

### 3.1 Datasheet basis

```
Iz = rated_current × k_temp × k_bundle
k_temp = round2( sqrt( (θmax − θpanel) / (θmax − θref) ) ), capped at 1.00 (Q6)
```

- `rated_current`, `θref` (`rated_at`) and `θmax` (`max_temperature`) come from the wire category
  (wires) or cable type (cables). For a cable, θpanel is the cable's ambient.
- θpanel ≥ θmax gives k_temp = 0 and the Error `rating-too-hot` ("+CAB1 at 70 °C is not below the
  conductor's 70 °C").
- θref ≥ θmax is refused when the style is written.
- The formula is the one from which IEC 60364-5-52's ambient factors are derived. A test checks that
  it reproduces the built-in ambient table to 2 dp (§5.4), which guards against typos in either.

### 3.2 Table basis

```
Iz = I_table(method, insulation, loaded, size) × k_ambient(insulation, θ) × k_group(arrangement, n)
```

- `loaded` is 2 for `ac1` and `dc`, 3 for `ac3` (the supply system, §6.3).
- `insulation` is `pvc` when θmax is 70, `xlpe` when θmax is 90. Any other θmax has no table
  (`rating-no-table`, "No IEC table for a 105 °C conductor; use Datasheet").
- `k_ambient` is the table's value at the next **higher** listed temperature when θ is between rows
  (conservative; 37 °C uses the 40 °C row). Above the last row: `rating-no-table`.
- `k_group` is the table's value for n circuits in the method's arrangement, the next **higher**
  listed count when between columns. n = 1 gives 1.00.
- Sizes not in the table (AWG, 25 mm² and up in the first set): `rating-no-table`.

### 3.3 Manual basis

`Iz = rating.manual`. No factors. The result says "set by hand".

### 3.4 The overload checks

With In from §4.1 and Ib from §4.3:

| Condition | Row |
| --- | --- |
| Iz < In | Error `rating-under` |
| Ib > In (only when a load is known, typed or from Power) | Warning `rating-load-over` |
| Ib > Iz and In ≥ Iz did not fire | (covered by the two above; no third row) |

Comparison uses the **unrounded** values. Display rounds Iz and factors as §3.6 says.

### 3.5 Volt drop (cables only; Q15)

```
R_θ  = R20(size, class) × (1 + 0.00393 × (θmax − 20))        Ω/km = mΩ/m, copper
mV/A/m = 2 × R_θ (ac1, dc)    or    √3 × R_θ (ac3)
ΔU   = mV/A/m × I × L / 1000                                  V
ΔU % = 100 × ΔU / U
```

- `R20` is IEC 60228's maximum DC resistance at 20 °C for the conductor class (§5.2). θmax is used,
  as the published mV/A/m tables do: conservative, and matches them.
- Reactance is ignored. That is sound for the first set's sizes (≤ 16 mm²), where the published
  tables give a resistive value only. A size above 16 mm² gives `rating-no-table` for volt drop.
- **I** is Ib when known, else In, and the note says which (Q3). No device and no load: no volt drop,
  Info `rating-no-current`.
- **L** is the cable's `length` (028), parsed (§6.1). No length: 028's existing no-length row; no
  extra row.
- **U** is the supply voltage (§6.3).
- ΔU % > limit: Warning `voltdrop-over`. This is **this cable only**, not the drop from the origin
  of the installation that BS 7671 Appendix 12 limits; the tooltip says so (Q10).

### 3.6 Rounding and display

- Factors are rounded to 2 dp **before** multiplying, because the tables publish 2 dp and the user
  must be able to redo the sum from the panel. (`27 × 0.87 × 0.80` is what the panel shows, so
  that is what is computed.)
- Iz is shown to 0.1 A, half up. ΔU % is shown to 0.1 %, half up, and to 0.01 % below 0.1 %.
- Comparisons use the unrounded products. A result within 0.05 A of In shows the extra digit
  (`4.04 A ≥ 4 A`), so a pass never reads as `4.0 A ≥ 4 A` against a fail that reads the same.

## 4. Inputs

### 4.1 The protective device and In

In comes from the protective device's own `rating` property (034), or `rating.<n>` for a fuse of a
fuse bank, or `rating.<position>` for a fuse position on a strip (036). The text is free
(`13A C 30mA`, `6.3A T`, `C16`). `parse::amps` reads, in order:

1. the first number immediately followed by `A` (spaces allowed, case-insensitive, not `mA` or `kA`):
   `13A C 30mA` → 13, `6.3 A T` → 6.3, `4A` → 4;
2. else a curve letter `B`, `C`, `D`, `K` or `Z` directly followed by a number: `C16` → 16;
3. else nothing: Info `rating-device-unread` ("-Q01's rating 'K' has no current; write it as 16 A").

A bare number (`16`) is **not** read: it may be a size or a curve, so the message asks for `A`. Values must be finite, > 0 and ≤ 6300 A; anything else is unread. At most the first
64 characters are scanned.

**Protective device kinds** (stop the trace): `mcb1`, `mcb2`, `rcbo2`, `fuse`, `fuseterm`,
`fusebank4`…`fusebank12`, and strip fuse positions (036). `rcd2` and `rcd4` are **not** overcurrent
devices: the trace crosses them (§4.2). A device whose kind is not in the list may be named by hand
with `protected_by` only if it has a readable rating; the command says "-K01 is not a protective
device kind; it is used because you named it" (Info row `rating-named-device`).

### 4.2 Which device protects a subject (trace)

Owner question Q2. Recommended rule:

1. **By hand:** the subject's `protected_by` (a device tag, or `-F01:3` for a fuse of a bank or a
   strip position) wins. A tag that names nothing gives the Warning `rating-device-missing`.
2. **Traced:** from the subject's net (a cable: each core's net; the result is the worst core),
   walk the connectivity graph breadth-first towards devices:
   - a net reaching a protective device's **load-side** pin stops there: that device protects it;
   - a net reaching a pass-through device crosses it from pin to paired pin: terminals and strip
     positions (same position), connector pins (same pin, other half), contacts of the same
     device by IEC 60947 pin pairs (1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 13–14, 21–22, …, and `L1–T1` forms), RCDs by
     pole;
   - any other device stops that branch.
3. The walk is bounded: at most `MAX_TRACE_NETS = 64` nets per subject, and each net is visited
   once (cycles end). Hitting the bound: Info `rating-no-device` with "trace stopped after 64
   nets; set Protected by".
4. Two or more devices found at the same depth: the **largest In** is used (it is the harder test
   for Iz ≥ In) and Info `rating-two-devices` names both.
5. Nothing found: Info count `rating-no-device` (only for subjects that have rating data, §7).

**Load-side pins** come from each protective starter's pin labels, and the worker writes them as a
table in `trace.rs`, checked by a test against `defaults/symbols.rs`: even-numbered pins (2, 4, 6)
of `mcb*`, `rcbo2`, `fuse`; the numbered outputs of `fuseterm` and fuse banks (`symbols::bank_fuses`
labels). If a starter's labels do not follow this, the worker stops and asks (do not guess).

The trace result per net is kept and patched (§10). It is never recomputed for the whole project
on an ordinary edit.

### 4.3 Load current: `LoadSource` (the interface for 0.2.3)

```rust
pub enum LoadFrom { Typed, Power }
pub struct Load { pub amps: f64, pub from: LoadFrom }
pub trait LoadSource {
    /// The running design current of a rated subject, if known.
    fn load(&self, doc: &Document, s: RatedSubject) -> Option<Load>;
    /// Which subjects' loads an op may change (for the update map).
    fn touched(&self, doc: &Document, op: &Op) -> Vec<RatedSubject>;
}
```

- 057 ships `TypedLoads`: the subject's own `load` property (A, §6.1).
- 0.2.3's Power brief adds `PowerLoads` (the load tree's running current per net) and composes
  `Typed → Power`: **a typed load wins** and shows `typed`; the Power value shows `from Power`
  (Q4). Peak and diversity stay in Power; 057 reads the running current only.
- Volt drop uses In when no load is known (Q3); the overload check Ib ≤ In runs only with a load.

### 4.4 Part data without a parts catalogue

There is no parts catalogue yet (roadmap 2.3). Typed datasheet values live where many conductors
share them, so one entry rates every wire of that kind, and 2.3 can replace them later.

**Panel wires: new fields on the wire category** (050's `wire` catalogue; FP3). The category
already holds the cross-section, part number and maker, so the rating sits beside the part it
describes:

| Field | Kind | Built-in | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rated_current` | Number 0.1–6300 (A), or none | none | Rated |
| `rated_at` | Number −40–90 (°C) | 30 | `at 30 °C` |
| `max_temperature` | Number 50–250 (°C) | 70 | Tooltip; picks the IEC table (70 PVC, 90 XLPE) |
| `material` | Choice `cu` | `cu` | Tooltip. Aluminium later (Q12) |
| `conductor_class` | Choice `1`, `2`, `5`, `6` | `5` | Tooltip; volt drop only |

The built-in categories keep `rated_current` none (no made-up numbers, as 050 Q5 did for the
allowance). Their `max_temperature` stays 70 (H05V-K / H07V-K are PVC 70 °C).

**Field cables: a minimal `cable` catalogue, cable types** (050 FP7 reserved the word; FP4). A
cable chooses one with the property `cable_type` (S P O choice entry, built-in `none`):

| Field | Kind | Built-in | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cross_section` | Number 0.05–1000 (mm²) | none (then the `type` notation, §2.2) | |
| `material`, `max_temperature`, `conductor_class`, `rated_current`, `rated_at` | as for wire categories | as for wire categories | |
| `part_number`, `maker`, `description` | Text ≤ 64 | empty | For 2.7 and the BOM later; 057 shows them only |

Roadmap 2.7 (cable manager) adds its fields (core notation, shield, pairs) to the same catalogue;
057 adds none of those. The cable's free `type` text (028) is unchanged and still drives the core
offer. "Change cable type…" on board 2.06 offers the catalogue entries first.

**Not part sets.** 047's part lines describe devices; a wire or cable is not a device, and a part
line has no place for a rating. 057 adds nothing to them.

**When 2.3 lands**, a category or cable type gains a `part` reference. Resolution becomes: the
object's Manual value → the part's data → the typed fields → none. Each value already carries its
source (`Resolved`), so the panel shows "from part CB-RNF-4G15" with no layout change. Typed
fields are kept, not converted, and a check can later list typed values that differ from the part.

**Panel temperature** is typed per enclosure: the location box's `rating.temperature` (FP6), else
the project's `rating.panel_temperature`. A wire takes the **highest** of its two ends' locations
(a wire between two enclosures is rare and the hotter one rules). The Power brief's Heat tab
(rev 4 "Power: heat") later supplies a computed inside temperature through the same function,
`panel_temperature(doc, subject) -> (f64, From)`, with `From::Typed | Project | Heat`. A typed value
wins, as for loads.

Kept small and correct. Copper only. Everything else gives `rating-no-table`, with a message
naming the basis to use instead.

### 5.2 Table identifiers and record

Each table in `tables.rs` is a `const` with:

```rust
pub struct TableSource {
    pub standard: &'static str,   // "IEC 60364-5-52:2009+A1"
    pub table: &'static str,      // "B.52.14"
    pub column: &'static str,     // "PVC"
    pub transcribed_by: &'static str,
    pub checked_by: Option<&'static str>,  // None until a second person checks it
    pub checked_on: Option<&'static str>,  // ISO date
}
```

A table with `checked_by: None` is **unverified**. Its results show `· unverified`, and one Info
row `rating-unverified` appears while any subject uses one: "IEC table values not yet checked
against the standard (B.52.14, …)". There is no switch to hide it.

### 5.3 Sourcing and verification (for a human)

The numbers must come from a licensed copy of each standard. They are not in this design.

1. The worker builds the table **structure** with test-fixture values (marked `FIXTURE` in the
   source and failing a release check if any remain, below).
2. A person with a licensed copy (Q11: the owner or an engineer they name) transcribes the values
   into `tables.rs`, citing standard, edition, table and column, and fills `transcribed_by`.
3. A **second** person checks every value against the standard and fills `checked_by` and
   `checked_on`. The PR shows the diff of the values only.
4. Cross-checks run as tests, so a typo cannot pass silently:
   - every ambient factor equals `round2(sqrt((θmax − θ)/(θmax − 30)))` for its column;
   - every capacity column rises strictly with size, and each 3-loaded value is below its 2-loaded
     value;
   - every grouping factor falls (or stays) as the count rises, and is 1.00 at one circuit;
   - every class's R20 falls strictly with size, and class 5 ≥ class 2 at each size;
   - the computed mV/A/m (§3.5) agrees within 1 mV/A/m with a short list of published BS 7671
     mV/A/m values that the checker types into the test (the checker, not the worker).
5. `cargo xtask` gains no new gate in this brief. The release checklist (027) gains one line: "no
   `FIXTURE` values and no unchecked rating table in a release build", checked by a test that runs
   under `--features release-check`. If the owner prefers a softer rule, Q11 decides it.

**Values that must be checked by a human** (all of them, but these are the ones this design's
arithmetic depends on): every capacity cell; the ambient factors at 35 °C and 40 °C; the grouping
factors for 2 and 3 circuits in arrangements 1 and 4; R20 for 0.75, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.5 mm² in classes
2 and 5; and the rev 4 board examples (Q16).

## 6. Values, bounds and defaults

### 6.1 Parsing (hostile input)

| Input | Accepts | Bounds | Otherwise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current (`load`, `rating.manual`, `rated_current`) | `3.2`, `3.2 A`, `3,2 A` | finite, 0 < x ≤ 6300 | refused on write; ignored with `rating-bad-value` in a file made elsewhere |
| Length (028 `length`) | `12`, `12 m`, `12.5m`, `1200 mm` | finite, 0 < x ≤ 10 000 m | no length (028's row) |
| Cross-section (category, type, conductor override) | `2.5`, `2.5 mm²`, `2.5mm2` | 0.05–1000 | `18 AWG` and others: no table (`rating-no-table`) |
| Temperature | `35`, `35 °C`, `35C` | −40 to 90 °C ambient; 50–250 °C θmax | refused |
| Factor (`rating.bundling`) | `0.8`, `3 bunched` | 0.30–1.00; count 1–20 | refused |
| Count (`rating.grouped`) | `3` | 1–20 | refused |
| Voltage | `400`, `400 V` | 1–1000 V | refused |

Every parser reads at most 32 characters. No parser indexes; all use `get` and checked arithmetic.

## 7. Checks

All in the existing Checks list (`checks.rs`). Every row has go-to, and a fix where one is safe.
Nothing is refused because of a check. Rows exist only for **rated subjects**: a conductor whose
category or own properties give rating data, or which has `rating.*` set; a cable with a cable
type, a parsable `type` size, or `rating.*` set. Plain signal wires with no data make no rows,
except the one count below (only where a device protects them), so a project that does not rate
wires is not nagged.

| Kind | Severity | Says | Go to | Fix |

| `rating-too-hot` | Error | `+CAB1 at 70 °C is not below L2-03's 70 °C` | the wire | **Show rating** |

## 8. Commands, JSON forms and MCP

Everything is a command. Programmatic calls never prompt.

### 8.1 `rating` (writes)

```json
{"wire": "L2-03", "basis": "datasheet", "bundling": "3 bunched", "load": "3.2 A", "protected_by": "-Q01"}
{"cable": "-W01", "basis": "table", "method": "e", "ambient": 35, "grouped": 3, "vd_limit": 5,
 "cable_type": "#<id>", "system": "ac3", "voltage": 400, "manual": null}
```

- One transaction of `SetProperty` ops. `null` clears a key; an absent key is untouched.
- Every value goes through §6.1; a refusal names the key and the range ("ambient is −40 to 90 °C").
- `protected_by` must name an existing device or fuse (`-F01:3`), else refused with the way on.
- `wire` accepts a conductor ID or a wire number; a number shared by several conductors applies to
  all of them (022's rule for `potential`). `many: [..]` sets several subjects in one transaction.
- Command line: `rating` prompts `Select wire or cable:` then `[Basis/Bundling/Load/Protected
  by/Method/Ambient/Grouped]`, as 050b's `category` does.

### 8.2 `ratings` (reads)

`{"wire": "L2-03"}`, `{"cable": "-W01"}` or `{}` (all rated subjects, paged 200). Returns, per
subject: basis, each factor with its value, input and source (`{"name": "panel", "factor": 0.87,
"input": "40 °C", "from": "location +CAB1"}`), Iz unrounded and shown, the device (tag, In, how it
was found: `named` / `traced`), the load and its source, the volt drop (V, %, current used and
why), the table sources and `verified`, and the check kinds raised. MCP agents read the same
object the panel shows.

### 8.3 MCP contract tests (brief 011 rule)

`rating`: one transaction, `Author::Agent`, refused with writes off, undone by one undo. `ratings`:
allowed with writes off.

## 10. Update maps

Rating results are kept per rated subject (`Key::Rating(s)` in Checks) with a kept index:

- `protects: device → subjects` and `path: net → subjects` from the trace (§4.2);
- `users: category / cable type → subjects` (050's users index serves);
- `at: location → subjects` (by the locations of their ends).

| Edit | Re-checks |
| --- | --- |
| A subject's own properties (`rating.*`, `load`, `protected_by`, `cable_type`, `cross_section`, `length`) | That subject |
| A protective device's `rating` / `rating.<n>` | `protects[device]` only |
| A connectivity change (wire drawn, joined, erased; a device placed or erased) | Subjects whose `path` contains a touched net; their trace is redone; and new subjects on the touched nets |
| A wire category or cable type `SetStyle` | Its users |
| A location's `rating.temperature`, or a device's location | `at[location]`; the moved device's wires |
| A project default in area `rating` | Every rated subject's **result** (not its trace). The bench row below bounds it |
| A load from Power (0.2.3) | `LoadSource::touched` |
| Anything else | Nothing |

The wires and cables tables patch the Status / Checks cell of re-checked rows only. No table is
rebuilt.

**Bench** (medium while building, large once): open the medium project with every power wire rated;
time to change `-Q01`'s rating, to draw one wire on a protected net, and to change
`rating.panel_temperature`. Budgets: the first two under 2 ms re-check; the default change under
the existing full-checks build time on the medium project.

## 11. Acceptance checks (tester; worked examples)

Run from outside (`cadcraft-cli`, control channel, MCP). Fixture tables are used where the
standard's value would otherwise be needed; the expected results follow from the fixture by the
formulas of §3, so the arithmetic is tested apart from the transcription.

**Fixtures** (in `tests/all/lanes/lane_057.rs`, never in `tables.rs`): capacity, method E, PVC,
3 loaded, 1.5 mm² = **23 A**; method E, PVC, 2 loaded, 0.75 mm² is absent; ambient PVC 35 °C =
**0.94**, 40 °C = **0.87**; grouping arrangement 4, 3 circuits = **0.89**; R20 class 5:
0.75 mm² = **26.0**, 1.0 mm² = **19.5**, 1.5 mm² = **13.3** Ω/km.

1. **Datasheet, board 2.05.** Category `Power L / N`: 2.5 mm², `rated_current 27`, `rated_at 30`,
   `max_temperature 70`. Location +CAB1 `rating.temperature 40`. Wire L2-03 `rating.bundling 0.80`,
   traced to -Q01 `rating "4A C"`. k_temp = round2(√(30/40)) = round2(0.8660) = **0.87**.
   Iz = 27 × 0.87 × 0.80 = **18.792** → shown **18.8 A**; 18.792 ≥ 4: pass, no row. `ratings` returns
   `panel 0.87 (40 °C, location +CAB1)`, `bundling 0.80 (set here)`, device `-Q01 4 A traced`.
2. **Same wire, -Q01 `rating "20 A"`.** 18.792 < 20 → Error `rating-under`: `L2-03 carries 18.8 A;
   -Q01 is 20 A`. The counter rises by one error. Undo: the row goes.
3. **IEC table, board 2.06.** Cable -W01, no cable type, `type "H07RN-F 4G1.5"` → 1.5 mm², assumed
   Cu / 70 °C / class 5 (one `rating-assumed` row). `rating {cable: "-W01", basis: "table", method:
   "e", ambient: 35, grouped: 3}`, `ac3`. Iz = 23 × 0.94 × 0.89 = **19.2418** → **19.2 A**; pass
   against 4 A. (The board shows 19.3: Q16.)
4. **Volt drop, same cable.** L = `12 m`, no load → I = In = 4 A, U = 400 V, ac3, θmax 70.
   R70 = 13.3 × (1 + 0.00393 × 50) = 13.3 × 1.1965 = **15.9135** mΩ/m. mV/A/m = √3 × 15.9135 =
   **27.563**. ΔU = 27.563 × 4 × 12 / 1000 = **1.3230 V**; ΔU % = **0.331 %** → shown **0.3 %**,
   note `at 4 A, -Q01 rating`; ≤ 5 %: pass.
5. **Volt drop over, 24 V DC sensor cable.** Cable -W12, cable type: 0.75 mm², class 5, 70 °C.
   `system dc`, `voltage 24`, `length "50 m"`, `load 0.5`. R70 = 26.0 × 1.1965 = **31.109**;
   mV/A/m = 2 × 31.109 = **62.218**; ΔU = 62.218 × 0.5 × 50 / 1000 = **1.5555 V** = **6.48 %** →
   Warning `voltdrop-over`: `-W12 drops 6.5 % at 0.5 A; limit 5 % (1.0 mm² would drop 4.9 %)`.
   Check of the suggestion: 19.5 × 1.1965 = 23.332; × 2 = 46.663; × 0.025 = 1.1666 V = 4.86 %.
   Set `vd_limit 7`: the row goes.
6. **Manual.** Wire L1-03 `rating {basis: "manual", manual: "10 A"}`, device -Q03 `16 A` → Error
   `rating-under`: `L1-03 carries 10 A; -Q03 is 16 A`. `manual: "0"`, `"-3"`, `"NaN"`, `"1e400"`
   are each refused with the range; nothing is written.
7. **Load over device.** -W01 `load 5`, -Q01 4 A → Warning `rating-load-over`. Volt drop now uses
   5 A: 27.563 × 5 × 12 / 1000 = 1.6538 V = 0.41 % → **0.4 %**, note `at 5 A, typed`.
8. **Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz all at once.** Manual Iz 3.5, -Q01 4 A, load 3: Error `rating-under` only (no
   load-over, 3 ≤ 4).
9. **Datasheet temperature cap and limit.** Panel 25 °C: √(45/40) = 1.0607 → capped **1.00**
   (Q6); Iz = 27 × 1.00 × 0.80 = 21.6. Panel 70 °C and 75 °C are both accepted (≤ 90 °C) and
   give Error `rating-too-hot`; 95 °C is refused with the range.
10. **Device rating parsing.** `rating` values and the In read: `4A C` → 4; `C16` → 16;
    `13A C 30mA` → 13; `6.3 A T` → 6.3; `16` → unread; `K` → unread; `0 A`, `-4 A`, `1e9 A`,
    `NaN A` → unread; a 10 000-character rating → unread, no hang. Each unread gives
    `rating-device-unread` once per device.
11. **Trace.** Sample-like page: -Q01 (mcb, pins 1→2) → -K01 (power3, 1–2, 3–4, 5–6) → L2-03 →
    -X01:2 → -W01 core 2 → -M01. L2-03 and -W01 both find -Q01 (`traced`). An RCD -Q10 between
    -Q01 and -K01 is crossed. A wire on -Q01's **line** side finds whatever feeds it, not -Q01.
    Two MCBs feeding one net (6 A and 10 A): 10 A used, `rating-two-devices`. A loop of 100 nets
    through terminals: `rating-no-device` with "trace stopped after 64 nets"; no hang.
12. **Protected by.** `protected_by "-Q09"` (absent) → refused by `rating`; the same key set by
    `setprop` in a file → Warning `rating-device-missing`. `protected_by "-F01:3"` on a fuse bank
    with `rating.3 "2 A"` → In 2 A.
13. **No data, no nagging.** A project with no rating data anywhere has no rating rows at all.
    Give one category a `rated_current`: rows appear only for its wires, plus `rating-no-data`
    counting the other protected wires.
14. **No table.** Cable type 25 mm², basis table → `rating-no-table`, fix **Use Datasheet** offered
    only if the type has `rated_current`. θmax 105 → `rating-no-table` ("No IEC table for a 105 °C
    conductor").
15. **Unverified.** With fixtures marked unchecked, exactly one `rating-unverified` row while any
    subject uses Table; none when all use Datasheet or Manual.
16. **Update map.** Medium project, counters on: changing -Q01's rating re-checks exactly
    `protects[-Q01]`; editing an unrelated text re-checks nothing; changing a cable type re-checks
    its users only; no table rebuild in any case.
17. **MCP contract** (§8.3), and `ratings` with writes off.
18. **Table self-checks** (§5.3 point 4) pass on the shipped tables.
19. **Screens.** Boards 2.05 and 2.06 side by side with `ui.screenshot` of the same states: the
    Rating sections' rows, order, notes and folded summaries match, apart from 19.2 (Q16).

## 14. Questions for the owner

Each has a recommendation.

1. **Cable datasheet values: a cable-type catalogue, or properties on each cable?** **Recommend
   the catalogue** (FP4): cables of one type share their data, 2.7 extends the same catalogue, and
   2.3 later points it at a part. Per-cable properties would have to be typed again on every cable.
2. **How the protective device is found.** **Recommend the trace of §4.2** (through terminals,
   connector pins, contact pin pairs and RCDs, to a protective device's load-side pin, bounded at
   64 nets), with `protected_by` as the override. The alternative, by hand only, is simpler but
   leaves most wires unchecked.
3. **Volt drop with no load set: use the device's In?** **Recommend yes**, labelled `at 4 A, -Q01
   rating`. It is conservative and matches board 2.06. Without it, nothing has a volt drop until
   0.2.3.
4. **A typed load against Power's (0.2.3).** **Recommend the typed load wins**, shown as `typed`,
   with Power's value in its tooltip.
5. **Rounding.** **Recommend factors to 2 dp before multiplying, Iz to 0.1 A, compare unrounded**
   (§3.6), so the panel's sum is what was computed.
6. **Datasheet basis in a panel cooler than the datasheet's reference: allow k > 1.00?**
   **Recommend a cap at 1.00.** A datasheet rating does not promise more in a cool panel. The
   Table basis keeps the table's factors above 1.00 as published.
7. **Bundling not set.** **Recommend × 1.00 with a Warning count**, not a made-up default. The
   alternative is a built-in 0.70 (3 bunched), which is safe but hides that nobody chose it.
8. **Panel temperature default 40 °C?** **Recommend yes**, shown as `project default`. It is above
   the 30 °C reference, so it errs safe, and Power ▸ Heat replaces it with a computed value later.
9. **Field ambient default 30 °C?** **Recommend yes**, shown as `project default`, because it is
   the tables' reference. Projects in hot places set it once.
10. **Volt-drop limit: 5 % per cable, this cable only?** **Recommend yes for 0.2.2**, with the
    tooltip "this cable only". The drop from the origin (BS 7671 App. 12: 3 % lighting, 5 % other,
    from a public supply) needs the load tree, so it waits for Power (0.2.3).
11. **Table data: licensing and verification.** House rule 9 (licensing). Standard tables are
    copyright of IEC and BSI. **Recommend:** ship only the small first set of §5.1 as numeric
    values with citations (no table layout, notes or text), transcribed and checked by two people
    with licensed copies, the owner naming them; 0.2.2 may ship with `rating-unverified` showing;
    0.3.0 requires every shipped table checked (the release-check test). Also: add 1.0 mm² from
    BS 7671? **Recommend not yet**; Datasheet covers panel control wiring.
12. **Copper only in the first set?** **Recommend yes.** Aluminium adds a choice value and tables,
    with no format change.
13. **Fault protection (k²S², earth-fault loop impedance, disconnection times) out of 0.2.2?**
    **Recommend out**, as a later brief. The overload check is what the boards show.
14. **Ctrl K quick sums** ("results that matter become checks"): **recommend a separate brief.**
    057 builds the calculation functions they would call.
15. **Volt drop on panel wires?** **Recommend no.** Board 2.05 shows none, and panel lengths make it
    negligible. `ratings` returns it for a wire if asked with `{voltdrop: true}`, for agents.
16. **The rev 4 board examples.** 23 × 0.94 × 0.89 = 19.24, which rounds to 19.2, not the 19.3 the
    board shows. Also, the example mixes values that may come from different insulation columns
    (0.94 at 35 °C is the 70 °C column's factor). **Recommend** correcting board 2.06 to 19.2 A and
    having the human checker confirm which column 23 A and 0.89 come from (§5.3).
17. **IEC 60364-5-52 or BS 7671 as the built-in source?** **Recommend IEC**, with BS 7671 as the
    cross-check (the values are harmonised for these tables). If the checker finds a difference,
    it is listed for the owner, and a `rating.table_source` choice can follow with no format change.

## 15. Out of scope

Fault protection (Q13). Volt drop from the origin and diversity (Power, 0.2.3). Aluminium. Methods
other than B1, B2, C and E; buried cables (method D) and multi-tray grouping (B.52.20). Sizes above
16 mm² and below 1.5 mm² in the tables. Single-core cables in trefoil. Flexible-cord tables (BS 7671
4F series); H07RN-F is treated by its insulation temperature, which the checker confirms. Harmonic
neutral currents. Ctrl K quick sums (Q14). The parts catalogue (2.3) and the cable manager (2.7).
Panel layout duct fill (rev 4 Draw: panel layout page). A calculator screen of any kind.
